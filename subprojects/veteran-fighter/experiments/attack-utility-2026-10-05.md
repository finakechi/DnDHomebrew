# Veteran Fighter — Attack-Action Utility Experiment Report

## Scope

This branch began with an experiment to explore Hard-Won Lessons that modify the Attack action without increasing damage.

The rules for the experiment were:

- Combat applications had to be primarily utility/control rather than damage.
- Defensive benefits had to remain minimal.
- Noncombat applications of the Attack action were encouraged to be broad, creative, and experimental.
- The experiment should avoid recreating the older rejected system where arbitrary actions could simply replace attacks.

## Initial Exploration

A large pool of combat utility concepts was generated, including:

- disarming;
- breaking grapples or forcing creatures to release objects;
- interfering with reactions;
- creating openings for allies;
- controlling enemy movement;
- suppression;
- manipulating equipment;
- controlling spaces and routes.

A much larger group of noncombat concepts was also explored, including:

- cutting ropes and restraints;
- manipulating distant mechanisms;
- probing dangerous areas;
- using weapons for leverage, hammering, cutting, anchoring, and rough repair;
- retrieving or moving objects;
- creating climbing aids;
- sending lines;
- interacting with doors, rigging, latches, and other mechanisms;
- precision ranged interactions;
- creating distractions and signals;
- delivering objects to allies.

This revealed that many seemingly different options were actually expressions of one concept:

> An experienced Veteran knows how to use a weapon as a practical tool rather than only as a means of inflicting damage.

## Field-Expedient Tool

Those weapon/tool concepts were consolidated into a single provisional Hard-Won Lesson:

### Field-Expedient Tool — Attack

When you take the Attack action, you can replace one of your attacks with a Utilize action that targets a nonmagical object or mechanism.

When you do so:

- you can use your weapon's attack ability modifier in place of the ability modifier normally used for any required check;
- you add your Proficiency Bonus if you are proficient with that weapon;
- you can perform the interaction at your weapon's reach;
- if using a ranged or thrown weapon, you can perform an appropriate interaction within that weapon's normal range.

This Lesson can't target an object worn or carried by an unwilling creature and doesn't replace a required Tool proficiency.

**Status:** Provisionally locked.

Its purpose is to cover cutting, levering, hammering, probing, manipulating mechanisms, triggering distant objects, rough repairs, and similar physical tasks without requiring separate mechanics for every use.

## Remaining Attack-Action Concepts

After removing the options absorbed by Field-Expedient Tool, the experiment focused on utility involving creatures, battlefield positioning, communication, and object transfer.

### Break Their Hold — Attack

Break Their Grip and Break a Grapple were combined.

Replace one attack to interfere with a creature within reach. The target makes a Strength or Dexterity saving throw against the Veteran's Unarmed Strike save DC.

On a failed save, the Veteran can cause it to:

- end one Grapple it is maintaining; or
- drop one chosen object it is holding.

This also absorbs the basic Disarm concept.

**Status:** Provisionally locked.

### Misdirect with Sound — Attack

Replace one attack to deliberately create a distracting noise at another location.

The intended asymmetry is:

- ranged or thrown weapons can create the sound within their normal range;
- a melee-focused Veteran can instead throw a small object, tentatively at a much shorter range such as 30 feet.

The proposed mechanical payoff is that creatures hearing the distraction without seeing its source become worse at determining where another creature actually is, such as suffering Disadvantage on an appropriate Perception check.

**Status:** Provisionally locked as a concept; exact wording remains open.

### Deliver an Object — Attack

Replace one attack to throw a small carried object to a willing creature within a bounded range.

The recipient can use its Reaction to catch the object if it has a free hand; otherwise, the object lands nearby.

Likely uses include:

- passing a potion;
- delivering ammunition;
- throwing someone a key;
- sending tools;
- passing a torch;
- delivering another needed piece of equipment.

Delivering an object does not also activate or use it.

**Status:** Provisionally locked.

## Other Attack Candidates Retained

### Open the Lane — Attack

Sacrifice an attack to help an ally reposition safely around an enemy.

**Status:** Experimental.

### Spoil the Reaction — Attack

Sacrifice an attack to interfere with or temporarily deny an enemy's Reaction.

This may be strong enough to need a level prerequisite or another restriction.

**Status:** Experimental.

### Suppressing Fire — Attack

A ranged battlefield-control option that discourages or complicates movement through a chosen area without increasing damage.

**Status:** Experimental.

## Current Attack-Action Shortlist

1. Field-Expedient Tool
2. Break Their Hold
3. Misdirect with Sound
4. Deliver an Object
5. Open the Lane
6. Spoil the Reaction
7. Suppressing Fire

The first four currently appear the most distinctive because they provide forms of utility that the normal Fighter chassis, Weapon Mastery, and Battle Master do not already cover particularly well.

## Design Conclusion

The experiment suggests that the Attack action has useful unexplored design space for the Veteran, provided the subclass is not rewarded with extra damage simply for choosing utility.

The strongest emerging principle is:

> Extra Attack can represent not only how quickly the Veteran attacks, but how quickly an experienced combatant can perform practical physical tasks during a fight.

This differs from the older rejected attack-replacement engine.

Instead of allowing arbitrary actions to replace attacks, each Hard-Won Lesson grants a specific, bounded procedure with its own purpose.

## Recommended Next Step

The project now has enough ideas. The next productive step is consolidation rather than further expansion.

Recommended order:

1. Create a single authoritative current-state document.
2. Build a complete Hard-Won Lesson matrix.
3. Classify every Lesson as Keep, Merge, Experimental, or Cut.
4. Decide which of the seven Attack candidates actually deserve permanent roster space.
5. Run hypothetical Lesson-selection tests across several Fighter builds.
6. Perform a full subclass integration pass.
7. Move into structured table playtesting.

The central design question has shifted from:

> Can the Attack action support interesting Veteran utility?

to:

> Which few of these options are strong and distinctive enough to earn permanent places in the Hard-Won Lessons roster?
