# Fighter Subclass: Veteran

Learn When the Proper Way Is the Wrong Way

A new fighter learns the proper way to fight. A Veteran has learned when the proper way is the wrong way.

Veterans are not necessarily master duelists, brilliant tacticians, or warriors of extraordinary strength. They are survivors. They have endured battles in which plans failed, formations broke, supplies ran short, and the difference between life and death was often an improvised solution.

Their hard-won experience makes weapons, fieldcraft, and practical improvisation parts of the same craft. They know when to strike, when to retreat, when to help another soldier, and when an ordinary tool or handful of dirt is more useful than another swing of a sword.

## Level 3: Hard-Won Lessons

Your experience has taught you techniques for surviving dangerous situations and making the most of whatever is available.

You learn three Hard-Won Lessons of your choice from the options presented at the end of this subclass. You must meet a Lesson's prerequisite to learn it.

You learn one additional Lesson at Fighter levels 7, 10, 15, and 18.

| Fighter Level | Lessons Known |
|---|---:|
| 3 | 3 |
| 7 | 4 |
| 10 | 5 |
| 15 | 6 |
| 18 | 7 |

A Lesson modifies only the action or Reaction it names unless it says otherwise. If multiple Lessons can apply to the same action, you can apply them together unless their effects require different choices for that action.

A Lesson doesn't grant an additional action, Bonus Action, or Reaction unless it expressly says so.

Attack Procedures. A Lesson that replaces an attack can be used only during an Attack action on your turn that isn't expressly limited to one attack. It replaces an attack granted by that action or your Extra Attack feature, not an extra attack granted by Practical Fighting or the Light property.

Each such Lesson can replace only one attack during that action. The procedure isn't an attack roll, deals no attack damage, and doesn't apply weapon properties or magical attack benefits except as the Lesson specifies.

## Level 3: Practical Experience

Your years of hard service have taught you skills beyond the use of weapons.

You gain proficiency in one of these skills of your choice: Animal Handling, Athletics, Insight, Intimidation, Investigation, Medicine, Perception, Stealth, or Survival.

Choose one skill from this list in which you are proficient; you gain Expertise in it.

## Level 3: Practical Fighting

You have learned to fight without relying on perfect technique, using whatever methods the situation demands.

You gain the following benefits.

### Practical Contact

You have proficiency with Improvised Weapons.

When you choose the Damage option of your Unarmed Strike, you can use Strength or Dexterity for its attack and damage rolls. You can replace its normal damage with 1d6 plus the chosen modifier.

A held weapon, equipped Shield, or similar implement can represent the Damage or Shove option of this Unarmed Strike. It remains an Unarmed Strike against a creature within 5 feet and gains none of the implement's weapon properties, mastery properties, or magical benefits.

This feature doesn't change the Grapple or Shove save DC or the requirements for those options.

### Practical Fighting Options

Once during each Attack action on your turn, immediately after you make an attack with a weapon, you can use one of the following options, as appropriate.

An attack granted by Close Work or Hit or Correct is part of that Attack action and can't trigger this feature again. You can't use this feature during an Attack action expressly limited to one attack.

Close Work. After a melee attack with a weapon, make an Unarmed Strike against a creature within 5 feet of you.

Hit or Correct. After a ranged attack with a weapon, move up to half your Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks. You can then make one ranged attack with a weapon.

If the triggering attack hit, this additional attack must target a different creature. If it missed, the additional attack can target the same creature or a different creature.

Don't add a positive ability modifier to the additional attack's damage. The weapon's normal requirements still apply.

Deliver. Throw one object you are holding, weighing no more than 5 pounds and small enough to hold in one hand, to a willing creature you can see within 30 feet.

The object must have an unobstructed path. The creature can take a Reaction to catch it if it can see the object and has a free hand. Otherwise, it lands in the creature's space.

This doesn't activate, use, drink, or equip the object.

Misdirect. Create a distracting sound at a point you can see by throwing a small nonmagical object up to 30 feet, or by striking an object or surface with a ranged or thrown weapon within its normal range.

The point must have an unobstructed path, and the method must be capable of producing an audible impact there. Expend the object or ammunition normally.

Until the start of your next turn, a creature within 30 feet of that point that heard the sound but couldn't see how you caused it has Disadvantage on the first Wisdom (Perception) check it makes to locate a creature other than you by hearing.

This doesn't conceal you, obscure anything, or prevent a noise you make from revealing you.

Deliver and Misdirect aren't attacks and deal no attack damage.

## Level 7: Battlefield Readiness

You have survived enough battles to recognize danger and prepare for it before the fighting begins.

When you roll Initiative, before making the roll, choose two different options below.

Counter-Ambush takes effect immediately. Resolve the other chosen options at the start of your first turn in that combat, in either order. If you have the Incapacitated condition at that time, you can't resolve them.

Counter-Ambush. Being surprised doesn't impose Disadvantage on this Initiative roll.

Take Position. Move up to half your Speed.

Read the Field. Take the Search or Study action.

Brace. Gain Temporary Hit Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus.

An action taken with this feature can benefit from a Hard-Won Lesson that modifies it.

## Level 10: Been Here Before

Experience has taught you to recognize familiar problems and adapt your methods to new circumstances.

You gain the following benefits.

Change of Approach. When you finish a Short or Long Rest, you can replace one Hard-Won Lesson you know with another for which you qualify.

Reserve Lessons. When you finish a Long Rest, choose two Hard-Won Lessons for which you qualify and that you don't know. They are your Reserve Lessons until you choose again.

If you learn one of them, it is no longer a Reserve Lesson; you don't choose a replacement until your next Long Rest.

When you begin an action, Reaction, or procedure that a Reserve Lesson can modify, you can use that Lesson as though you knew it for that action, Reaction, or procedure. Declare this use before making any of its rolls.

The Lesson's resulting effects last for their normal duration, including a readied action or assistance that resolves later, but you gain no continuing access to the Lesson. Any proficiency it grants applies only to that use.

Once you use either Reserve Lesson this way, you can't use this benefit again until you finish a Short or Long Rest.

This benefit doesn't reset or bypass a Lesson's own limits.

## Level 15: Still in the Fight

You have survived setbacks that would have broken less experienced warriors. Even when a battle turns against you, you know how to recover and keep fighting.

You gain the following benefits.

Reserve Readiness. Once per combat, at the start of one of your turns after your first turn, you can use one Battlefield Readiness option you haven't used in that combat, other than Counter-Ambush.

You can't use this benefit while Incapacitated.

Dig Deep. When you use Action Surge, you can expend one use of Second Wind to regain its Hit Points without taking a Bonus Action.

You can also move up to half your Speed without provoking Opportunity Attacks, as with Tactical Shift.

Improved Brace. Your Brace option grants Temporary Hit Points equal to twice your Proficiency Bonus.

## Level 18: Veteran of a Hundred Fights

Few threats are entirely unfamiliar to you. You have faced desperate enemies, survived their deadliest attacks, and learned to recognize the openings they leave behind.

At the start of each of your turns in combat, choose one creature you can see.

Until the start of your next turn, you have Advantage on attack rolls against it and saving throws it forces you to make, and it has Disadvantage on attack rolls against you.

## Hard-Won Lessons

The following Lessons are available to Veterans. Each Lesson enhances a particular action or Reaction, representing techniques learned through experience rather than formal training.

You must meet a Lesson's Fighter-level prerequisite to learn it.

### Attack Lessons

#### Break Their Hold

Once during an Attack action on your turn, you can replace one attack to interfere with a creature within 5 feet of you, or within the reach of a Melee weapon you are holding and use for the procedure.

The creature makes a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (its choice) against your Unarmed Strike save DC.

On a failed save, choose one grapple it is maintaining to end, or one unsecured object it is holding to drop.

A dropped object lands in its space. This can't remove worn or attached equipment, and it doesn't override a feature that prevents disarming.

#### Field-Expedient Tool

Once during an Attack action on your turn, you can replace one attack with a Utilize action to perform one physical procedure on a nonmagical object or mechanism using a weapon you are holding: cutting, levering, hammering, probing, applying pressure, or rough repair.

The procedure must normally be possible with one Utilize action. It can't target anything worn or carried by an unwilling creature.

For a required ability check, you can use the ability modifier you would use to attack with that weapon. You add your Proficiency Bonus if you are proficient with it, without adding the bonus more than once.

You must still have any tools or tool proficiency the procedure requires.

You can perform the procedure within the weapon's melee reach. With a ranged or thrown weapon, you can instead strike a point within its normal range along an unobstructed path, expending ammunition or throwing the weapon normally.

A ranged procedure must be accomplished by that strike's impact, cutting, or pressure; it can't perform fine manipulation or use tools at a distance.

This Lesson doesn't deal attack damage, use or apply a consumable, don or doff equipment, craft an item, accelerate a procedure lasting longer than an action, or activate a magic item.

A Hard-Won Lesson that modifies Utilize can apply if the procedure also meets that Lesson's requirements.

### Dash Lessons

#### Break Through

When you take the Dash action, until the end of the turn you can move through an enemy's space if it is no more than one size larger than you.

Your movement doesn't provoke Opportunity Attacks from enemies whose spaces you enter this way.

The first time you enter each such enemy's space on that turn, it makes a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (its choice) against your Unarmed Strike save DC.

On a failed save, you can push it 5 feet to an unoccupied space adjacent to its original space.

You can't willingly end your movement in another creature's space.

#### Forced March

When you take the Dash action, gain 10 additional feet of movement from that action, and Difficult Terrain doesn't cost you extra movement until the end of the turn.

### Disengage Lessons

#### Casualty Extraction

When you take the Disengage action, until the end of the turn you don't spend extra movement to drag or carry a willing creature or one with the Unconscious condition.

You must still be able to physically move the creature.

#### Parting Blow

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 7

When you use your Action to take the Disengage action, you can make one attack with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike at any point during your movement on that turn.

On a hit, the target's Speed is reduced by 10 feet until the start of your next turn.

Multiple uses of this Lesson don't increase that reduction on the same target.

### Dodge Lessons

#### Hold Ground

When you take the Dodge action, until the start of your next turn you have Advantage on saving throws and ability checks to avoid becoming Prone, becoming Grappled, or being moved against your will.

Forced movement that allows you no saving throw or ability check is reduced by 10 feet, to a minimum of 0.

Having your Speed reduced to 0 by the Grappled condition doesn't itself end the benefits of that Dodge action. Its other limitations still apply.

#### Shield the Line

When you take the Dodge action, choose one willing creature within 5 feet of you.

Until the start of your next turn, it has Half Cover while it is within 5 feet of you and you aren't Incapacitated.

### Help Lessons

#### Fire-Team Assist

When you take the Help action to assist against an enemy, choose one of these options:

Distract. The enemy can be within 30 feet of you instead of 5 feet.

Until the start of your next turn, your allies have Advantage on attack rolls against it until the first allied attack hits it.

After that hit, the attacker can immediately move up to 10 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks from that enemy.

Two-Man Takedown. If the enemy is within 5 feet of you, choose Grapple or Shove.

Before the start of your next turn, the next time one of your allies uses that option of an Unarmed Strike against it, the enemy has Disadvantage on the saving throw.

This replaces the Help action's normal attack-roll assistance.

#### Veteran's Correction

When an ally fails an ability check that received Advantage from your Help action, you can use Tactical Mind on that check as though you had failed it.

The normal Second Wind cost and Tactical Mind rules apply.

### Hide Lessons

#### Field Concealment

When you take the Hide action, ignore Disadvantage on its Dexterity (Stealth) check imposed by your armor.

After you succeed on that check, you can choose up to your Proficiency Bonus willing creatures within 30 feet that can see or hear you.

For 10 minutes, you and the chosen creatures have Advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made to Hide and ignore armor-imposed Disadvantage on those checks.

Normal requirements for hiding still apply.

Once you grant this group benefit, you can't do so again until you finish a Short or Long Rest.

### Influence Lessons

#### Common Soldier's Tongue

When you take the Influence action against a Bloodied creature that can understand you, you can urge it to surrender, retreat, drop a held weapon, release a creature, or cease fighting.

If the DM determines that the demand is a plausible means of survival for it, treat its willingness as Hesitant unless it is already Willing.

Its attitude doesn't change. This can't override magical compulsion or make an impossible or suicidal demand feasible.

You also have Advantage on Influence checks about danger, supplies, routes, pay, security, working conditions, or survival when dealing with soldiers, guards, mercenaries, sailors, laborers, and similar practical workers.

Conversation can reveal ordinary relevant information they know and aren't trying to conceal; it doesn't compel disclosure of secrets.

### Magic Lessons

#### Battlefield Activation

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 10

When you take the Magic action to activate a magic item, you can draw or stow that item as part of the action.

After the activation resolves, you can make one attack with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike as a Bonus Action on that turn.

The item's normal activation and eligibility requirements apply.

### Opportunity Attack Lessons

#### Hook and Turn

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 7

When you hit a creature with an Opportunity Attack, the attack deals an extra 1d10 damage of a type it deals.

If the target is Large or smaller, you can also move it up to 5 feet to an unoccupied space within the reach you used for that attack.

### Ready Lessons

#### Hair Trigger

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 7

When you take the Ready action to ready the Attack action and use your Reaction to take that action, you can make two attacks with weapons or Unarmed Strikes instead of one.

This becomes three attacks at Fighter level 11 and four at Fighter level 20.

Once during that readied action, when you hit with one of these attacks, you can cause it to deal an extra 1d10 damage of a type it deals.

This Lesson extends only the number of attacks granted by your Fighter Extra Attack feature; other features keep their own requirements and limits.

### Search Lessons

#### Threat Scan

When you take the Search action to look for concealed creatures, immediate physical dangers, tracks, or signs of recent violence or an impending ambush, you have Advantage on its Wisdom check.

On a success, the DM can identify one useful tactical detail supported by what you found.

If that Search reveals a concealed creature's location, choose one creature found this way.

Until the start of your next turn, the first attack roll made against it has Advantage, and the first attack roll it makes has Disadvantage.

### Study Lessons

#### Find the Weak Point

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 10

When you take the Study action to examine a creature or nonmagical object, structure, vehicle, or mechanism you can observe, you can look for a practical weakness.

The DM determines whether one can be inferred from the available evidence and chooses the relevant Intelligence check and DC.

On a success, the DM identifies that weakness and how an attack or physical procedure could exploit it.

For 1 minute, once on each turn, you or a creature that can see or hear your directions can have Advantage on one attack roll or ability check that exploits the weakness in the described way.

This benefit is shared among all such creatures.

It ends early if the weakness no longer applies or you successfully identify another weakness with this Lesson.

The information can include damage, structural condition, failure points, or possible repair and bypass methods.

It doesn't reveal numerical game statistics or unsupported damage vulnerabilities.

Useful information remains valid after the Advantage ends until the subject changes.

### Utilize Lessons

#### Quick Barricade

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 7

When you take the Utilize action while within reach of sturdy materials you can physically manipulate, you can arrange them into a barricade along up to two connected 5-foot edges within reach.

The materials must be sufficient to obstruct attacks.

The barricade grants Half Cover against attacks and effects passing through it.

Crossing one of its protected edges costs 5 extra feet of movement.

Its AC, Hit Points, and other properties are those of its actual materials.

Outside combat, you can spend 10 minutes assessing a camp, room, doorway, chokepoint, or firing position and planning or arranging practical fortifications.

Larger work uses the normal materials, checks, and time the DM determines; this Lesson doesn't create materials or shorten construction beyond what is physically feasible.

#### Sabotage

Prerequisite: Fighter Level 7

While you know this Lesson, you have proficiency with Thieves' Tools or Tinker's Tools, chosen when you learn it.

When you take the Utilize action to interfere with an accessible nonmagical object or mechanism, you have Advantage on any required check.

The DM determines the appropriate check, tools, and feasibility.

On a success, choose one of these effects:

Jam. One normal function can't be used until the sabotage is cleared.

Rig. The object appears functional, but one normal function you choose fails the next time it is used. The sabotage then becomes obvious.

Choose one method when you begin:

Hasty. The attempt takes one Utilize action. After making the attempt, you can make one attack with a weapon or an Unarmed Strike as a Bonus Action on that turn.

A creature can clear the sabotage with a Utilize action and an ability check using an appropriate skill or tool, against DC 8 plus your Proficiency Bonus and your Strength or Dexterity modifier, chosen when you sabotage it.

Deliberate. The attempt takes 1 minute.

Clearing it requires suitable tools or materials and a check against the same DC; simply removing an obstruction isn't sufficient.

The attempt must plausibly interfere with the object's construction or operation.

This Lesson can't make it fail in an unsupported way, override a magic item's properties, or replace the normal rules for damaging objects.
