# 2. Project identity and design goals

**Subclass:** Veteran  
**Class:** Fighter  
**Ruleset:** D&D 2024 / 5.5e

The Veteran represents an experienced front-line soldier, campaigner, survivor, guard, mercenary, sailor, caravan hand, scout, or similarly practical combatant.

Its power should come from:
- accumulated experience;
- fieldcraft;
- preparedness;
- situational awareness;
- adaptability;
- practical violence;
- dirty tricks;
- equipment use;
- timing;
- knowing where to stand;
- knowing what breaks;
- recognizing familiar danger;
- and changing methods when the proper technique fails.

The Veteran is **mundane**. It should not rely on spells, pseudo-magic, supernatural explanations, command authority, or heroic/noble status.

It should not collapse into:
- Battle Master with more maneuvers;
- Rogue with Fighter numbers;
- Ranger without magic;
- Bard with soldier flavor;
- Cavalier/Sentinel lockdown;
- Eldritch Knight without spells;
- Banneret/Warlord/officer identity.

### Priority order

1. Distinct Veteran identity and satisfying gameplay.
2. Correct 2024/5.5e rules interaction and clean action economy.
3. Strong enough martial power at all tiers.
4. Meaningful Lesson/build choices.
5. Noncombat utility and second-best-role flexibility.
6. Concision and table usability.
7. Exact numerical tuning after the mechanical identity works.

### Strong design preferences

- Closed, bounded mechanics over open-ended “ask the DM if my dirty trick works.”
- Practical information should usually be qualitative rather than stat-block extraction.
- Campaign-niche Lessons are acceptable if genuinely strong when relevant.
- Feat synergy is good unless it creates imbalance, mandatory optimization, rules confusion, or unintended action compression.
- Utility should matter at the table rather than being decorative ribbon text.
- Unarmed Strike builds should remain supported where practical.
- The Veteran should be able to become a credible “second-best” stealth, scouting, social, sabotage, or magic-item user through selected Lessons without stealing another class's core engine.

# 5. Hard-Won Lessons

**Status:** accepted/stable for now, but still open to critical audit.

Lessons known:
- Fighter 3: 4
- Fighter 7: 5
- Fighter 10: 6
- Fighter 15: 7
- Fighter 18: 8

Governing principle:

> Unless a Lesson says otherwise, it modifies only the action or Reaction named in the Lesson and doesn't change that action or Reaction into another.

Whenever the Veteran gains a Fighter level, one known Lesson can be replaced with another qualified Lesson.

There are currently **14 Lessons**.

## Level 3-accessible Lessons

### Forced March — Dash
- Dash.
- +10 feet of movement associated with the action.
- Difficult Terrain costs no extra movement until the end of the turn.

### Break Contact — Dash
- Dash.
- Choose one visible creature.
- The Veteran's movement does not provoke Opportunity Attacks from that creature for the turn.

### Field Concealment — Hide
- Armor-imposed Stealth Disadvantage does not apply to the Hide-action check.
- On success, choose up to PB willing creatures within 30 feet that can see/hear the Veteran.
- For 10 minutes, the Veteran and selected creatures have Advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks made as part of Hide and ignore armor-imposed Disadvantage on those checks.
- Does not waive normal Hide requirements.
- The 10-minute benefit is once per Short or Long Rest.

### Shield the Line — Dodge
- When the Veteran Dodges, choose a willing creature within 5 feet.
- That creature has Half Cover while it remains within 5 feet until the start of the Veteran's next turn and while the Veteran is not Incapacitated.

### Hold Ground — Dodge
While benefiting from Dodge:
- Advantage on saves/checks to avoid being knocked Prone or moved against the Veteran's will.
- If forced movement permits no save/check, reduce the distance by 10 feet, minimum 0.

### Fire-Team Assist — Help
- Ability-check Help can reach an ally within 30 feet if communication is possible.
- Attack Help can target an enemy within 15 feet instead of normal close range.
- If the helped attack hits, the attacker may immediately move 5 feet without provoking from the creature hit.

### Threat Scan — Search
- When using Search to locate a creature, gain Advantage if the target moved, attacked, or made a clearly audible sound since the end of the Veteran's previous turn.
- Success may reveal the direction of recent movement/attack when the evidence permits.
- If multiple creatures are located, choose one.
- The first attack by the Veteran or an ally who can see/hear the Veteran against that chosen target before the Veteran's next turn has Advantage.

## Level 7 Lessons

### Hair Trigger — Ready
Prerequisite: Fighter 7.
- The Veteran takes Ready and readies the **Attack action** using a weapon or Unarmed Strike.
- When the trigger occurs and the Veteran spends the Reaction to take the readied Attack action:
  - make two attacks;
  - at Fighter 15, make three attacks.
- Once during that readied Attack action, a hit deals +1d10 damage.

Important established rules correction:
- Fighter Extra Attack grants its extra attacks when the Attack action is taken **on the Fighter's turn**.
- A typical readied Attack action off-turn therefore makes one attack unless Hair Trigger explicitly changes the count.
- Do not assume Ready inherently receives normal Extra Attack progression.

### Hook and Turn — Opportunity Attack
Prerequisite: Fighter 7.
On an Opportunity Attack hit:
- +1d10 damage;
- if the target is Large or smaller, move it up to 5 feet to an unoccupied space within reach.
- The movement does not have to be directly away.

## Level 10 Lessons

### Common Soldier's Tongue — Influence
- Advantage on qualifying Influence checks involving people with substantial practical occupational experience, such as military personnel, guards, mercenaries, dangerous manual laborers, caravan workers, shipboard workers, or comparable practical professions.
- The approach must concern relevant practical matters such as danger, supplies, routes, pay, working conditions, security, fortifications, logistics, or survival.
- No magical compulsion and no impossible Influence attempts.

### Battlefield Activation — Magic
- Uses the Magic action specifically to activate a magic item, including one whose activation casts a spell.
- Can draw/stow the item as part of the action.
- After the activation resolves, can make one weapon or Unarmed Strike attack as a Bonus Action that turn.
- Does not waive item requirements.
- Does not change the activation action.
- Does not permit the Magic action through Action Surge.

### Sabotage — Utilize
- Advantage on Utilize checks to disable, jam, wedge, tangle, break, or obstruct a nonmagical object or mechanism.
- Reversible sabotage may require an action plus a check to reverse.
- Suggested DC structure: 8 + PB + Strength or Dexterity modifier selected by the Veteran.
- Obvious bypass methods can still work automatically.

### Quick Barricade — Utilize
- Use nearby sturdy movable objects or suitable loose material.
- Form a temporary barricade along the edge of a 5-foot space within reach.
- Grants Half Cover through the protected edge.
- No supernatural or special durability.

### Find the Weak Point — Study
- Study a nonmagical object, structure, vehicle, fortification, mechanism, or equipment within 60 feet.
- A successful Intelligence check identifies a plausible weakness/failure point.
- The first attack/check exploiting it before the end of the Veteran's next turn gains Advantage.
- Cannot invent unsupported weaknesses.

# 6. Change the Plan

**Status:** provisional current Fighter-3 combat feature.

Working wording:

> **Change the Plan**  
> When one approach fails, you know how to turn the opening into something useful.  
>  
> Once during each **Attack action you take on your turn**, you can use one of the following responses:  
>  
> **Weapon to Unarmed.** Immediately after you miss a creature with a melee weapon attack, you can make an **Unarmed Strike** against that creature.  
>  
> **Unarmed to Weapon.** Immediately after a creature succeeds on its saving throw against the Grapple or Shove option of your Unarmed Strike, you can make one **melee weapon attack** against that creature.  
>  
> An attack or Unarmed Strike made by this feature can't trigger Change the Plan again.

Current intentional playtest ruling:
- the generated weapon attack retains normal Weapon Mastery functionality.

If playtesting shows that is too strong, the **first proposed pullback** is to prevent the generated weapon attack from using a Mastery property. That restriction is **not currently active**.

### Intended identity
- proper technique fails;
- Veteran immediately changes methods;
- weapon ↔ body;
- strike ↔ grapple/shove;
- practical adaptation rather than formal technique.

### Known watchpoints
- Graze can make a miss productive before Change the Plan adds an Unarmed Strike.
- Fighter 13 Studied Attacks can grant Advantage on the next attack after a miss; the immediate Change the Plan attack may become that next attack.
- Grappler can interact with the follow-up Unarmed Strike.
- Action Surge may allow another Change the Plan sequence because the trigger is once during each Attack action.
- Need to evaluate whether this creates meaningful fail-forward play or simply attack multiplication.
- Need to evaluate ranged-Veteran support because the present wording is melee-centric.

# 7. Battlefield Readiness

**Status:** accepted level-7 direction.

At the start of the Veteran's first turn in each combat, choose **two different Battlefield Readiness options**.

Established options:

### Take Position
Move up to half Speed.

### Sweep
Take the Search action.

### Assess
Take the Study action.

### Brace
Available at Fighter 15.
Gain Temporary Hit Points equal to twice PB.

Actions taken through Battlefield Readiness can benefit from a known Veteran Lesson that enhances that action.

Battlefield Readiness is once per combat.

### Known concern
If the old Size Up mechanic is gone, **Assess** may be much less attractive before Find the Weak Point becomes available at Fighter 10. At Fighter 7–9, players may default to Take Position + Sweep.

Do not preserve a weak Assess option solely because an older feature depended on it.

### Later candidate discussion
Later discussion explored additional opening-combat options.
- **Counter-Ambush** was regarded favorably.
- **Press the Advantage** was rejected as too powerful.
- **Exploit the Confusion** was proposed as the preferred replacement for Press the Advantage, but no explicit final acceptance is recorded in the retained context.

Treat those as candidate design history unless a newer source establishes otherwise.

# 8. Been Here Before

**Status:** retained with an unresolved structural concern.

## Recalled Lesson
When taking an action or Reaction that could benefit from a Veteran Lesson the Veteran does not know:
- may use an unknown qualified Lesson as though known;
- declare before a required attack roll/ability check;
- cannot bypass or reset that Lesson's own use limits;
- once per Short or Long Rest.

## Change of Approach
After a Short or Long Rest:
- replace one known Lesson with another qualified Lesson.

### Known concern
Recalled Lesson may make niche Lessons **less likely to be permanently selected**, because a player can learn evergreen Lessons and rely on Recalled Lesson for unusual situations.

This concern remains unresolved.

# 9. No Lesson Wasted

**Status:** mostly retained but incomplete because Finish the Wounded was removed.

Retained elements:
- after a Short or Long Rest, replace **any number** of known Lessons with qualified Lessons;
- when Initiative is rolled, before the first turn, choose one unknown qualified Lesson;
- know that temporary Lesson until combat ends;
- Battlefield Readiness gains **Brace: 2 × PB Temporary Hit Points**.

The old clause increasing Finish the Wounded to 2d10 is obsolete and should not return.

There is **no accepted level-15 upgrade to Change the Plan**.

Do not silently convert the level-15 hole into:
- extra attacks;
- extra damage;
- additional Change the Plan uses;
- extra Reactions;
- or a Mastery rule change.

Those remain design questions to research.

# 10. Veteran of a Hundred Fights

**Status:** accepted level-18 concept.

Working concept:

> At the start of each of your turns in combat, choose one creature you can see. Until the start of your next turn, you have Advantage on attack rolls against that creature and on saving throws it forces you to make, and its attack rolls against you have Disadvantage.

Intended qualities:
- proactive;
- one creature at a time;
- only protects/enhances the Veteran, not allies;
- no extra Reaction;
- no special movement;
- no Read Threat state;
- intentionally strong against one high-priority opponent.

Known watchpoint:
- because attack Advantage lasts until the start of the Veteran's next turn, it can also apply to off-turn attacks such as Hair Trigger, Hook and Turn, and normal Opportunity Attacks.
- This was recognized as a playtest concern but not preemptively restricted.
- A possible later restriction is to grant attack Advantage only **on the Veteran's turn**, if necessary.

The old multi-part capstone involving Nothing New Under the Sun / Read the Threat / Punish the Mistake is superseded for excessive complexity.

# 11. Prior Deep Research conclusions

An earlier Deep Research report reached these broad conclusions:

- The Veteran concept is worth pursuing.
- The best architecture was the enhanced-generic-action / selectable-drill model that evolved into Hard-Won Lessons.
- The attack-replacement Veteran Actions engine should be abandoned, though isolated ideas could be salvaged.
- Do not make utility actions DPR-neutral with Attack.
- Do not use a broad “use utility → gain escalating damage” engine.
- Avoid free generic actions, especially Ready, Dodge, and Magic, without strict bounds.
- Avoid broad Reaction multiplication.
- Applied Mastery was rejected because it overlapped Tactical Master and enabled excessive mastery stacking.
- Cheap Sentinel-style packages were rejected.
- Magic Action support should be extremely narrow, preferably magic-item-specific if included.
- Recommended Lesson progression was approximately **4 / 5 / 6 / 7 / 8** known.
- Strongest utility niche: military analysis, fieldcraft, battlefield Search/Study, practical Help, gear/sabotage, preparedness.
- Biggest identity risk: becoming Battle Master with a larger maneuver list or Rogue actions on a Fighter.
- Biggest mathematical risk: reaction multiplication and free full actions.
- Biggest usability risk: trying to make every generic action equally important in combat.

Later follow-up research also corrected several overly conservative assumptions:
- Opportunity Attack interaction is not inherently problematic while normal Reaction limits remain; evaluate the actual effect.
- A modest rider once per **Attack action** is not automatically excessive merely because Action Surge can create another Attack action.
- Full-action Lessons can be allowed meaningful power because they carry real opportunity cost.
- Do not treat every feat synergy as a red flag.

The new research is free to disagree with any of these conclusions if current evidence supports doing so.

# 12. Major historical design paths and why they were rejected/superseded

## Veteran Actions / attack-replacement engine
Older versions let the Fighter replace attacks with generic actions or take Veteran Actions through special action-compression rules.

Rejected because:
- complex action economy;
- weak before Extra Attack unless compensated;
- drove escalating patches;
- risked making utility actions DPR-neutral;
- created an unnecessary parallel action subsystem.

## Tactical Blow
Utility action → escalating bonus damage.

Rejected because:
- turned utility into a damage-button prerequisite;
- encouraged actions for the wrong reason;
- distorted action valuation.

## Finish the Wounded
Once per Attack action, deal +1d10 to an already-injured target; later +2d10.

Mechanically workable but rejected/superseded because:
- passive;
- boring;
- did not express the Veteran's unique gameplay identity strongly enough.

## Veteran's Eye
Combined skill proficiency, Field Assessment, and Size Up.

Superseded because:
- too much word count for a ribbon;
- felt like multiple features stapled together;
- Size Up was itself a Study-action enhancement and therefore fit the Lesson architecture better than the ribbon slot.

## Vigilant Response
Reaction attack when an enemy missed the Veteran.

Superseded because:
- stronger Battlefield Readiness was preferred for the level-7 power budget;
- theme overlapped other retaliation mechanics.

## Field-Expedient Fighting
Improvised weapon and improved Unarmed Strike package.

Rejected as a core level-10 direction because:
- too narrow;
- displaced the stronger adaptability identity later represented by Been Here Before.

## Applied Mastery
Rejected because:
- overlapped Tactical Master;
- risked mastery stacking;
- duplicated base Fighter territory.

## Broad extra-Reaction engines
Rejected because reaction multiplication creates significant action-economy and off-turn damage/control risk.

## Open-ended Dirty Trick
Rejected because it creates negotiation burden and abuse potential.

## Read Threat / Punish the Mistake capstone
Powerful but rejected because:
- too many moving pieces;
- chained into other subclass mechanics;
- too much state tracking;
- simplified Veteran of a Hundred Fights better expresses the intended capstone fantasy.

## Press the Advantage
Later Battlefield Readiness candidate.
Rejected as too powerful.

# 13. Important 2024 Fighter interaction assumptions already established

The research should verify these against current official rules rather than blindly trust them, but these were the project's working assumptions:

- Fighter has Weapon Mastery at level 1.
- Tactical Mind is part of the level-2 Second Wind package.
- Tactical Shift arrives at Fighter 5.
- Tactical Master arrives at Fighter 9.
- Studied Attacks arrives at Fighter 13.
- Fighter Extra Attack scales upward at later levels.
- Tactical Master already supplies substantial mastery flexibility, so Veteran mechanics should not casually duplicate it.
- Studied Attacks already rewards a miss with future Advantage, creating a notable synergy with Change the Plan.
- Unarmed Strike is a normal attack option but does not inherently have a Weapon Mastery property.
- Ready can prepare the Attack action, but ordinary Fighter Extra Attack is tied to taking the Attack action on the Fighter's own turn, hence Hair Trigger's explicit off-turn multiattack count matters.
- Action Surge has restrictions relevant to the Magic action, so Veteran magic-item mechanics must not create a loophole.
