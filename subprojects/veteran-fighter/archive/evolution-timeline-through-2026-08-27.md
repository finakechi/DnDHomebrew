# PROJECT EVOLUTION TIMELINE

### Version 1 — Iteration A: Veteran Actions / Attack-Replacement Engine

**State:** Superseded

**Key state:**

- Core subsystem called **Veteran Actions**.
- Generic actions such as Dash, Help, Hide, Search, Study, and Utilize were inserted into or substituted for the Attack action.
- Early revisions tried:
  - replacing one attack with a Veteran Action;
  - taking one as a Bonus Action before Extra Attack;
  - later replacing attacks once Extra Attack was available.
- Higher levels added Ready, Influence, Magic, Dodge, multiple Veteran Actions, and eventually a free Veteran Action.
- **Tactical Blow** rewarded using a Veteran Action with extra weapon/Unarmed Strike damage:
  - 1d8 at Fighter 3;
  - 2d8 at 15;
  - 3d8 at 18.

**Changes / decisions:**

- Established the Veteran fantasy around fieldcraft, dirty tricks, timing, practical gear use, battlefield awareness, and mundane experience.
- Explored most of the 2024 generic actions as possible subclass hooks.

**Why changed:**

- The attack-replacement engine became increasingly wordy and difficult to price.
- It created nested-action and action-compression problems.
- Tactical Blow encouraged using utility mainly to recover DPR.
- Ready, Dodge, and Magic became particularly problematic when compressed into attacks.

**Ideas considered:**

- Veteran's Eye — Proposed/retained for later versions.
- Improved Help — Proposed/retained conceptually.
- Tactical Blow — Adopted temporarily, later rejected.
- Improvised fighting — Proposed/retained for later exploration.
- Extra/repeated Reactions — Proposed, later heavily restricted/rejected.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Generic **Veteran Actions** engine — excessive action-economy complexity.
- Free or attack-replacement Magic/Ready/Dodge — too difficult to balance cleanly.
- Tactical Blow as “utility action → damage” engine — distorted utility incentives.

### Version 2 — Iteration B: Veteran Tactics Menu

**State:** Superseded

**Changed from Version 1:**

- Converted many abilities into selectable **Veteran Tactics** rather than giving every Veteran every option.
- Used prerequisites to delay stronger options.
- Improved presentation and character differentiation.

**Why:**

- A selectable menu was easier to read than one enormous Veteran Actions feature.
- Different Veterans could specialize in different roles.

**New ideas considered:**

- Broad known-tactics progression, approximately **4 / 6 / 8 / 10 / all** — Proposed.
- Prerequisite-gated tactics — Adopted conceptually.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- The underlying Iteration A action-replacement engine remained, so its major problems remained.
- 4 / 6 / 8 / 10 / all progression — later judged too broad; “breadth is power.”
- “Veteran Tactics” terminology — later replaced first by Drills, then Lessons.

---

### Version 3 — Iteration C: Enhanced Generic Action Drills

**State:** Foundational architecture; superseded as a literal draft but still structurally important

**Changed from Version 2:**

- Abandoned the global attack-replacement system.
- Reframed each selectable option as a self-contained enhancement to an existing action or Reaction.
- Typical structure became:
  - “When you take the [specific action], [specific enhancement].”
- Renamed the selectable options **Veteran Drills**.
- Reduced recommended known progression to approximately:
  - Fighter 3: 4
  - Fighter 7: 5
  - Fighter 10: 6
  - Fighter 15: 7
  - Fighter 18: 8.
- Targeted roughly **10–14 printed Drills**.

**Why:**

- Local rules were easier to read, adjudicate, balance, and implement than a global action-conversion engine.
- Reduced overlap with Rogue Cunning Action and Battle Master.
- Preserved utility-action opportunity cost instead of trying to make every action DPR-neutral.

**New ideas considered:**

- Dash, Help, Search, Study, Utilize as especially promising Drill categories.
- Tightly scoped Ready and Opportunity Attack Drills.
- Narrow Influence and magic-item options.
- Veteran's Eye as question-based fieldcraft.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- **Applied Mastery** stacking Push/Sap/Slow on another Mastery — conflict with Fighter Tactical Master.
- Broad reaction multiplication — too risky.
- Full Sentinel-style package — too much lockdown.
- Open-ended Dirty Trick system — too adjudication-heavy.
- Exact enemy-stat extraction from Study — rejected.
- Huge “martial spellbook” Drill list — rejected.

### Research Checkpoint — Veteran Fighter Design, Balance, and Optimization Report

**State:** Major design roadmap

**Changes / decisions:**

- Deep Research selected **Iteration C** as the best architecture.
- Explicitly recommended abandoning Iteration A's engine while salvaging useful individual ideas.
- Established major constraints:
  - no generic action-replacement engine;
  - no utility-to-DPR conversion engine;
  - no careless Magic-action compression;
  - no broad extra-Reaction engine;
  - no Applied Mastery stacking;
  - avoid Rogue/Battle Master/Ranger/Bard/Cavalier/Eldritch Knight identity collapse.
- Recommended approximately **4 / 5 / 6 / 7 / 8** known options.

### Version 4 — Iteration D: First Full Drill-Based Draft

**State:** Superseded

**Changed from Version 3:**

- Produced the first full subclass using Iteration C as the base.
- **Veteran Drills** became the central customization system.
- Added a separate modest combat-pressure feature rather than Tactical Blow:
  - **Finish the Wounded**.
- Veteran's Eye returned as core fieldcraft.
- Developed a curated Drill roster drawing from earlier salvageable ideas.
- Included level-gated Ready, Opportunity Attack, social, equipment, sabotage, and other options.

**Why:**

- Needed a playtestable implementation of the research roadmap rather than another architectural sketch.

**New ideas considered:**

- Finish the Wounded.
- Size Up.
- Hair Trigger.
- Hook and Turn.
- Field-Expedient Fighting.
- Battlefield Readiness/readiness concepts.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Old Veteran Actions and Tactical Blow did not return.

### Version 5 — Iteration D.1: Less-Conservative Combat Revision

**State:** Superseded

**Changed from Iteration D:**

- Follow-up research challenged several earlier balance concerns as overstated.
- **Finish the Wounded** deliberately remained once per separate Attack action rather than once per turn.
- Modest extra triggers through multiple Attack actions, including Action Surge, were treated as acceptable.
- Opportunity Attack interaction was no longer treated as inherently dangerous.
- **Hook and Turn** and other full-action/Reaction options were allowed more power.
- **Size Up** was strengthened and folded into the subclass baseline.
- **Spot the Shooter** was removed as a standalone Drill because its value was too narrow.

**Why:**

- Numerical/interaction review suggested the prior research had sometimes been overly conservative.
- A feature interacting with Opportunity Attacks was not itself a balance problem while normal Reaction limits remained.
- A once-per-Attack-action d10 was not automatically excessive.

**New ideas considered:**

- Stronger Search/Study payoffs.
- More meaningful full-action Drills.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- **Spot the Shooter** — too little value for a standalone selectable option.

---

### Version 6 — Iteration D.2: Secondary-Role Expansion

**State:** Superseded as full version; much of its Lesson roster survives

**Changed from D.1:**

- Explicitly embraced the philosophy that a Veteran could become a credible **second-best** helper in another specialty without acquiring that class's core engine.
- Restored/added:
  - **Field Concealment — Hide**
  - **Battlefield Activation — Magic**
- Expanded to exactly **14 printed Veteran Drills**.
- Kept Hide and Magic options full-action-based and structurally bounded.

**Why:**

- Campaign-dependent options were considered acceptable if powerful in their intended campaigns.
- The Veteran should be able to shore up missing specialists without becoming Rogue, Bard, Ranger, or spellcaster.

**New ideas considered:**

- Group infiltration support.
- Magic-item-heavy campaign specialization.
- Campaign-niche Drills as legitimate known-option choices.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Blanket exclusion of Hide or Magic merely because another class specializes in them.

---

### Version 7 — Iteration D.3: Adaptability / Been Here Before

**State:** Superseded, but **Been Here Before** remains current

**Changed from D.2:**

- Removed **Field-Expedient Fighting** from level 10.
- Added **Been Here Before**:
  - **Recalled Lesson/Drill:** temporarily use an unknown qualified option once per Short or Long Rest.
  - **Change of Approach:** swap one known option after a Short or Long Rest.
- Level 15 adaptability was expanded so it would grow naturally from the level-10 concept.

**Why:**

- Field-Expedient Fighting's improvised-weapon/unarmed package overlapped Tavern Brawler/Unarmed Fighting and did not fit every Veteran.
- Adaptability better supported campaign-niche selections by giving the Veteran an emergency answer when their permanent specialization was wrong.

**New ideas considered:**

- Improvised fighting preserved only as a possible selectable option rather than universal subclass feature.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- **Field-Expedient Fighting** as universal level-10 feature — poor fit and overlap.
- **Hard Knuckles** / generic improved unarmed damage — not sufficiently appropriate as a universal Veteran feature.

---

### Version 8 — Iteration D.4: Hard-Won Lessons and Restored Battlefield Readiness

**State:** Superseded by D.4.1/D.4.2, but several structural choices remain

**Changed from D.3:**

- Restored **Battlefield Readiness** as a core level-7 feature rather than making readiness compete for a selectable slot.
- Renamed:
  - **Veteran Drills** → **Veteran Lessons**
  - level-3 feature → **Hard-Won Lessons**.
- Reason for naming change: “Drill” increasingly sounded like formal combat training and fit Common Soldier's Tongue, Battlefield Activation, Sabotage, etc. poorly.
- Battlefield Readiness options:
  - Take Position;
  - Sweep;
  - Assess.
- Added/proposed **Brace** at Fighter 15 to give Battlefield Readiness a fourth option.
- Level-18 Battlefield Readiness upgraded from one choice to **three different choices**.
- Developed the multi-part capstone:
  - Nothing New Under the Sun;
  - Read the Threat;
  - Punish the Mistake.

**Why:**

- Battlefield Readiness was considered too central to Veteran identity to become an optional Lesson.
- Broader “Lessons” terminology better covered combat techniques, habits, tricks, knowledge, and experience.
- Brace prevented the level-18 “choose three” wording from automatically meaning “get all available options.”

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Battlefield Readiness as a selectable Lesson — too universal/identity-defining.
- “Veteran Drills” terminology — replaced by Lessons.

### Version 9 — Iteration D.4.1: Rules/Timing Cleanup

**State:** Superseded by D.4.2 and later structural changes

**Changed from D.4:**

- Formalized **Hard-Won Lessons** and the 14-Lesson roster.
- Clarified **Finish the Wounded**:
  - once during each Attack action on the Veteran's turn;
  - separate Attack actions can each trigger it.
- Clarified **Recalled Lesson** timing:
  - choose before relevant attack roll/ability check;
  - cannot bypass/reset a Lesson's own usage limit.
- Clarified **No Lesson Wasted** temporary Lesson timing.
- Battlefield Readiness explicitly once per combat.
- Tightened **Hair Trigger** to two attacks, three at Fighter 15.
- Reworked Read the Threat timing from loose “once per round” toward a between-turns interval.
- Capstone retained:
  - Battlefield Readiness choose three;
  - Finish the Wounded requirement removal;
  - Read the Threat;
  - Punish the Mistake.
- Full D.4.1 subclass saved as **Veteran.md**.

**Why:**

- Wording/rules-clarity pass intended to preserve mechanics while reducing accidental RAW readings.

### Version 10 — Iteration D.4.2: Concision and Rules-Language Pass

**State:** Latest full displayed draft before subsequent redesign; now partially superseded

**Changed from D.4.1:**

- Removed redundant explanatory text around **Finish the Wounded** after user noted that “Attack action” versus “an attack” is sufficiently standard terminology.
- Corrected discussion of **Ready and Extra Attack**:
  - Ready can prepare the Attack action;
  - Fighter Extra Attack applies when taking the Attack action **on your turn**;
  - therefore a typical off-turn readied Attack normally makes one attack;
  - Hair Trigger explicitly creates its own two-/three-attack exception.
- Tightened Recalled Lesson for actions with and without rolls.
- Further condensed Veteran's Eye, Battlefield Readiness, Vigilant Response, and Lesson wording.
- Corrected Fire-Team Assist wording around ordinary Help requirements.
- Clarified Hook and Turn's movement.

**Why:**

- User requested a professional wording/rules-clarity pass without mechanical redesign.
- User specifically corrected over-explanation of Attack-action terminology and questioned prior Ready/Extra Attack analysis.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Redundant explanation that Action Surge permits a second Finish the Wounded trigger — removed from rules text as unnecessary.
- Earlier confusion suggesting Ready inherently gives the full Extra Attack sequence — corrected. The official Fighter rules tie Extra Attack to taking the Attack action on the Fighter's turn.

### Version 11 — Post-D.4.2 Structural Review: Level 3/7/18 Reassessment

**State:** Transitional; major decisions adopted

**Changed from D.4.2:**

User identified three disliked features:

- **Finish the Wounded** — powerful but boring.
- **Vigilant Response** — mechanically acceptable but thematically weak and too similar to Berserker-style retaliation.
- **Veteran of a Hundred Fights** — powerful enough but too bloated.

**Adopted changes:**

- **Vigilant Response removed.**
- Level-7 **Battlefield Readiness** strengthened:
  - choose **two different options** rather than one.
- Old level-18 multi-part capstone replaced in concept by a single proactive feature:
  - at the start of each turn, choose one visible creature;
  - Veteran has Advantage on attacks against it;
  - Veteran has Advantage on saves it forces;
  - its attacks against Veteran have Disadvantage;
  - effects last until start of Veteran's next turn.

**Why:**

- Stronger Battlefield Readiness reinforced a uniquely Veteran feature rather than adding a generic counterattack.
- Simplified capstone expressed one coherent idea: instantly reading one high-priority enemy.
- Removed Read Threat state, movement rider, Punish the Mistake, and Finish-the-Wounded capstone dependency.

**Ideas considered:**

- Tactical reaction movement replacing Vigilant Response — Proposed, not selected.
- Reaction Search/Study — Proposed, not selected.
- Bounded extra Reaction at 18 — reconsidered but not selected.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- **Vigilant Response** — theme mismatch/similarity.
- Old **Veteran of a Hundred Fights** package — too many features stapled together.
- Level-18 Battlefield Readiness “choose three” upgrade — superseded by clean capstone.

### Version 13 — “Tool at Hand / Whatever Works” Combat Concept

**State:** Superseded as exact implementation; thematic breakthrough retained

**Changed from Version 12:**

- User reframed **Right Tool for the Fight**:
  - not “carry/swap to the correct weapon”;
  - instead **“the tool you have is the best tool for the job.”**
- Examples:
  - professional photographer using the phone already in their pocket rather than the DSLR;
  - mechanic hammering a Torx bit into a stripped hex fastener because the improper solution works.
- Developed theme of:
  - unconventional weapon use;
  - pommel/haft/crossguard/levering;
  - using weapon, body, kicks, headbutts, shoulder checks, etc.;
  - “if it's stupid but it works, it's not stupid.”

**Ideas considered:**

- Forgo Weapon Mastery for +1d10.
- Alter physical damage type.
- Parallel unarmed alternatives based on giving up Grapple/Shove flexibility.
- Failed Grapple/Shove dealing damage.
- Choosing opponent's Strength or Dexterity save.
- Eye Gouge / Cheap Shot causing short-duration Blind.
- Weapon-assisted Grapple.
- Mixed weapon/unarmed sequences.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Simple “forgo Mastery → +1d10” as whole feature — not unique/interesting enough.
- Straight unarmed bonus damage — did not preserve the meaningful trade.
- Mandatory alternating attack sequence — poor at Fighter 3–4 before Extra Attack.
- Literal Eye Gouge as entire feature — interesting ingredient, but Blinded consumes substantial power and literal anatomy creates unnecessary issues.

### Version 14 — Change the Plan Prototype

**State:** **Current provisional combat-feature state**

**Changed from Version 13:**

- Online/historical research shifted the concept from “use the weapon incorrectly for bonus damage” toward **immediate transition when one method fails**.
- **Change the Plan** selected provisionally.

**Current working mechanic:**

- Once during each **Attack action taken on the Veteran's turn**, use one of:
  - **Weapon to Unarmed:** after missing a creature with a melee weapon attack, immediately make an Unarmed Strike against it.
  - **Unarmed to Weapon:** after a creature succeeds on its saving throw against the Grapple or Shove option of the Veteran's Unarmed Strike, immediately make one melee weapon attack against it.
- An attack or Unarmed Strike created by Change the Plan cannot trigger Change the Plan again.
- The follow-up weapon attack currently retains **full Weapon Mastery use**.

**Why:**

- Captures the “whatever works” identity without becoming another maneuver menu.
- Uses normal weapon attacks, Unarmed Strikes, Grapple, and Shove rather than inventing numerous special attacks.
- Historical research on Fiore-style integrated combat supported changing immediately from sword work to hilt/pommel work or grappling when the first approach failed.

**Ideas considered:**

- Remove Mastery from generated weapon attack — **Unresolved fallback**, not currently active.

**Rejected / superseded:**

- Earlier Mastery-for-damage and Cheap Shot packages as primary feature.
- The project is no longer searching for another passive damage-trigger replacement unless Change the Plan fails.
