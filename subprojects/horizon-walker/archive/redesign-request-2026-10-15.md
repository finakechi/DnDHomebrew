# Comprehensive Research and Redesign: Horizon Walker Ranger for D&D 2024 (5.5e)

## 1. Role and Primary Objective

Act as an expert Dungeons & Dragons 5th Edition rules designer, tabletop RPG systems analyst, and experienced homebrew developer specializing in mechanical balance, subclass design, and the revised 2024 D&D rules.

Your task is to conduct an extensive, evidence-based investigation and produce a **complete, playable, mechanically balanced, and thematically compelling revision of the Horizon Walker Ranger subclass**, originally published in *Xanathar's Guide to Everything* (2017).

The revised subclass must be designed specifically for the **2024 Player's Handbook Ranger**, also commonly called the 5.5e Ranger.

This is a comprehensive subclass modernization project, not a minor rewording or numerical adjustment.

**Primary design objectives, in priority order:**

1. Address the 2024 Ranger's excessive mechanical dependence on *Hunter's Mark*.
2. Fundamentally expand and improve the original *Detect Portal* feature.
3. Modernize and improve every other Horizon Walker subclass feature.
4. Preserve and strengthen the subclass's distinctive planar exploration, dimensional manipulation, teleportation, and supernatural combat identity.
5. Achieve mechanical balance with official 2024 Ranger subclasses while maintaining clarity, usability, and satisfying character progression.

The existing homebrew reports and playtest documents supplied with this request are **reference material, not authoritative final designs**. Challenge their assumptions, verify their calculations, investigate their shortcomings, and consider genuinely new solutions.

Your goal is to produce the strongest defensible revision, not merely combine previous drafts.

## 3. Core Design Problem: Hunter's Mark Dependency

This is the most important mechanical requirement.

I strongly dislike how deeply *Hunter's Mark* is integrated into the 2024 Ranger's base-class progression.

The spell's repeated mechanical promotion restricts character-building choices, competes with other concentration spells, and encourages Rangers to use the same combat routine regardless of their subclass identity.

**The revised Horizon Walker must meaningfully address this problem throughout the entire character progression.**

### Required class-feature audit

Investigate every base Ranger feature that directly references, modifies, grants, or depends upon *Hunter's Mark*, including:

| Ranger level | Feature | Required investigation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Favored Enemy | Free casts, resource value, and alternative uses |
| 13 | Relentless Hunter | Concentration protection and alternatives |
| 17 | Precise Hunter | Accuracy benefits and non-Mark equivalents |
| 20 | Foe Slayer | Capstone scaling and subclass compatibility |

Also examine the 2024 version of the *Hunter's Mark* spell itself.

Identify any additional interactions introduced by official rules, relevant feats, weapon properties, and Ranger subclasses.

### Explore multiple design approaches

Develop and compare at least three mechanically distinct approaches, including:

**Approach A — Hunter's Mark Transformation**

The Horizon Walker modifies *Hunter's Mark* into something more thematic, flexible, or mechanically distinct, potentially involving planar effects, dimensional tracking, force damage, or teleportation.

**Approach B — Independent Alternative**

The Horizon Walker develops a subclass-specific system that offers a competitive alternative to *Hunter's Mark*, allowing the Ranger to concentrate on other spells without becoming mechanically obsolete.

**Approach C — Integrated Class-Feature Replacements**

The subclass explicitly modifies or replaces the Ranger's Mark-dependent features at the appropriate character levels, establishing a different but comparable progression.

You may propose a hybrid approach or additional alternatives if they are demonstrably better.

### Mandatory evaluation criteria

Evaluate each proposal for:

- Damage output and consistency.
- Concentration opportunity costs.
- Bonus Action competition.
- Spell-slot and free-cast resource efficiency.
- Scaling at levels 13, 17, and 20.
- Interaction with multiclassing.
- Compatibility with the Ranger's existing features.
- Ease of understanding and use at the table.
- Thematic coherence.
- Potential unintended combinations or exploits.

**Every Hunter's Mark-dependent Ranger feature must receive an explicit mechanical treatment in the final design.**

A strong solution should allow the Horizon Walker to function effectively without being compelled to concentrate on *Hunter's Mark* in every important encounter.

The final mechanics should preserve meaningful choices, not merely replace one mandatory spell or repetitive routine with another.

If altering base-class features is necessary, clearly distinguish subclass-specific replacement rules from changes that would affect every Ranger.

Do not assume that preserving every published Ranger feature unchanged is more important than meeting the design objective.

## 4. Major Redesign: Detect Portal

The original *Detect Portal* is too narrow, too situational, and insufficiently rewarding for a subclass supposedly specialized in understanding the boundaries between planes.

I want this feature **substantially expanded**, potentially under a new, more appropriate thematic name.

### Required thematic scope

Investigate abilities involving:

- Detection of portals, gates, planar rifts, and dimensional breaches.
- Awareness of nearby planar boundaries, intersections, and overlaps.
- Detection of extradimensional spaces and magical spatial anomalies.
- Recognition of teleportation and interplanar travel occurring nearby.
- Identification or investigation of lingering dimensional disturbances.
- Recognition of unusual planar energy or spatial instability.
- Knowledge of the planes, their environments, their inhabitants, and their hazards.
- Identification of planar signatures and the likely nature of particular phenomena.
- Understanding of teleportation restrictions, planar barriers, and dimensional interference.

Examine whether some limited awareness of extraplanar creatures would be appropriate, with clear distinctions between detecting a creature and detecting a dimensional phenomenon.

### Suggested structure

Consider a layered feature with multiple complementary functions:

1. **Passive Sensitivity:** Limited but frequently useful awareness of nearby dimensional phenomena.
2. **Active Investigation:** Deliberate examination that reveals more specific information.
3. **Planar Knowledge:** Thematic skill proficiencies, knowledge bonuses, or unique opportunities to interpret planar evidence.
4. **Advanced Exploration:** Additional practical utility that develops through later Ranger levels, if justified.

These are starting concepts, not mandatory final mechanics.

### Design requirements

Establish precise rules for:

- Detection ranges.
- Action requirements.
- Resource limitations, if any.
- Detection through barriers.
- Active versus inactive phenomena.
- Recent teleportation traces.
- Magical concealment.
- Antimagic interactions.
- What information the Ranger learns automatically.
- What information requires an ability check or further investigation.

Balance practical usefulness against the risk of creating an unrestricted universal magical detection feature.

**Success criterion:** This feature should make a Horizon Walker feel like an expert planar investigator in ordinary exploration and mystery-solving adventures, rather than becoming useful only when the DM places a conventional portal nearby.

It should deliver satisfying noncombat utility without automatically solving entire mysteries.
