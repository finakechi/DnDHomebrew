**BARTITSU ROGUE**

Homebrew Subclass Design Report

*D&D 5.5e / 2024 Rogue*

A record of the design process, discarded and deferred options, balance reasoning, historical inspiration, and the current provisional subclass.

*Primary historical reference: “Bartitsu: Historical Overview and Design Foundations” (user-supplied research report).*

# 1. Executive Summary

The subclass began as a search for a Rogue that could deliver Sneak Attack with Unarmed Strikes without becoming a Monk analogue. Research into Bartitsu provided the strongest thematic foundation: an eclectic, practical self-defence system combining cane fighting, boxing, kicking, throws, joint manipulation, range control, deception, and the exploitation of an opponent’s commitment.

The design subsequently broadened from Unarmed Strikes alone to Unarmed Strikes plus Improvised Weapons, and then settled on a signature Cane as the subclass’s preferred tool. The current structure uses the Rogue’s existing Sneak Attack, Cunning Strike, Improved Cunning Strike, Cunning Action, Weapon Mastery, and Uncanny Dodge rather than introducing a separate resource pool.

The current provisional gameplay loop is: create an opening with Sucker Punch, exploit misses with a reaction attack, use the Cane’s Sap mastery to encourage enemy misses, apply Cunning Strike effects more efficiently from level 9, and at level 13 turn even successful enemy hits into counterattacks through Uncanny Dodge.

## Current Design Pillars

- Precision over volume: the subclass remains a one-good-hit Rogue rather than an Extra Attack or Flurry-style martial.

- Cane, body, and environment: the Cane is preferred, Unarmed Strikes are always available, and Improvised Weapons preserve adaptability.

- Create and exploit openings: feints, invitations, misses, and overcommitment drive the subclass’s offensive rhythm.

- Leverage over strength: Dexterity supports grappling and shoving, while Cunning Strike carries trips, locks, disarms, and repositioning.

- Rogue-native mechanics: Sneak Attack and Cunning Strike remain the core resource systems; no new point pool is added.

# 2. Historical Inspiration and Translation

The supplied Bartitsu research report was used as the historical design basis. The report characterizes canonical Bartitsu as an integrated system built from British boxing, Savate, Japanese jujutsu/jujitsu, and Pierre Vigny’s walking-stick method. Its recurring tactical themes include disturbing equilibrium, controlling distance, exploiting commitment, using leverage instead of strength, transitioning between ranges and methods, and responding pragmatically to unexpected street violence.

The subclass intentionally translates those principles rather than attempting a literal historical simulation. For example, generalized fantasy Improvised Weapon use is broader than the best-documented historical curriculum, but it expresses the same practical idea of remaining dangerous with ordinary objects. Likewise, the subclass avoids mystical martial-arts mechanics.

## Historical Themes That Survived Into the Mechanics

| **Bartitsu Theme**       | **D&D Translation**                                    | **Current Feature**                    |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| Invitation / feint       | Manufacture Advantage against a nearby foe             | Bartitsu Training: Create an Opening   |
| Exploit overcommitment   | Reaction attack when a nearby melee attack misses      | Bartitsu Training: Exploit an Opening  |
| Walking-stick method     | Signature Cane with dedicated statistics               | Cane                                   |
| Leverage over strength   | Dexterity-based Grapple/Shove DC                       | Bartitsu Training                      |
| Joint and weapon control | Disarm, Redirect, Joint Lock, Suppress                 | Scientific Leverage                    |
| Defense into offense     | Uncanny Dodge also permits a counterattack             | Counterstroke                          |
| Technique chaining       | Reduced Cunning Strike costs and three effects at once | Scientific Leverage / Perfect Leverage |

# 3. Design Evolution

## 3.1 Initial Theme Exploration

Several thematic bases were considered before Bartitsu became the primary inspiration:

| **Concept**                           | **Useful Elements**                                                                                 | **Disposition**                                                        |
|---------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Operative / Close-Quarters Specialist | Weaponless infiltration, precision violence, Fairbairn-style practical fighting.                    | Not kept as primary theme; retained as supporting tone.                |
| Bartitsu Gentleman / Gentleman Thief  | Cane fighting, eclectic ranges, urban self-defence, respectable appearance concealing combat skill. | Selected as the primary historical/theme base.                         |
| Gutter Fighter / Ruffian              | Dirty fighting, bottles, chairs, low kicks, unfair tactics.                                         | Not kept as primary theme; too generic and already common in homebrew. |
| Anatomist / Nerve-Striker             | Explains high Sneak Attack damage through anatomical precision rather than raw striking force.      | Retained as secondary flavour, especially for Cunning Strike.          |
| Enforcer / Collector                  | Grappling, intimidation, debt-collector or guild muscle fantasy.                                    | Not kept; leaned too heavily toward brute/Strength identity.           |

## 3.2 From Unarmed Rogue to Integrated Bartitsu

1.  The original mechanical premise was simply to let Unarmed Strikes trigger Sneak Attack.

2.  Improvised Weapons were added after Bartitsu research showed that ordinary objects and practical adaptation fit the style better than an unarmed-only chassis.

3.  The solution was simplified by having Unarmed Strikes and Improvised Weapons count as Finesse weapons for the subclass, rather than separately rewriting Dexterity attacks and Sneak Attack eligibility.

4.  The Cane was then elevated from a flavour example to a dedicated subclass feature and signature item.

5.  The Cane’s own Finesse property was removed; it instead counts as an Improvised Weapon for the practitioner and therefore inherits Finesse from Bartitsu Training.

## 3.3 Sucker Punch Development

Sucker Punch began as a narrow first-round or pre-combat idea, but the design goal shifted toward a repeatable combat engine. The final level-3 concept combines an active feint with a reactive counter.

| **Option Considered**            | **Mechanic**                                                                                       | **Why It Was Not Kept as the Final Standalone Version**                                                                                  |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| First-round sucker punch         | Benefit only against a creature that did not expect combat / had not acted yet.                    | Too narrow for a core subclass feature and risked overlapping Assassin territory.                                                        |
| Attitude-based sucker punch      | Key off Friendly/Indifferent/Hostile attitude or whether the target regarded the Rogue as hostile. | Too dependent on DM adjudication and social-state interpretation.                                                                        |
| Sneak Attack qualification only  | Bonus Action lets Sneak Attack apply without its normal setup.                                     | Often redundant because Rogues already qualify for Sneak Attack easily; Advantage stays useful even when Sneak Attack already qualifies. |
| Bonus Action Grapple/Shove setup | Use a Bonus Action Unarmed Strike to Grapple or Shove, then attack.                                | Strong Bartitsu feel but pushed toward Brawler/Monk territory and duplicated control already available through Cunning Strike.           |
| Change-Up / alternating modes    | Reward alternating Unarmed and Improvised Weapon attacks across turns.                             | Too much bookkeeping for a Rogue that normally makes one main attack.                                                                    |
| Literal Surprise rider           | Make the first Sucker Punch free when the creature was Surprised.                                  | Considered, but omitted from the current version to keep the feature concise and universally useful.                                     |
| Reaction counter on miss         | Enemy misses in melee; Reaction attack with Advantage.                                             | Kept and merged with the active feint into Bartitsu Training.                                                                            |

# 4. Options Not Currently Used

The following ideas were discussed or implied during development but are not part of the present subclass. Some were rejected outright; others remain potential material for future revision.

## 4.1 Mechanics Deliberately Avoided

- Martial Arts die or scaling Unarmed Strike damage: rejected to avoid rebuilding the Monk inside Rogue.

- Bonus Action attack chains / Flurry-style attacks: rejected; the subclass should remain focused on one precise Rogue attack.

- Extra Attack: rejected for the same reason and because Sneak Attack already supplies the class’s main damage progression.

- Unarmored Defense: rejected as unnecessary Monk overlap.

- Ki, stamina, or a new Bartitsu resource pool: rejected in favour of Sneak Attack dice, Cunning Strike, Bonus Actions, and Reactions.

- Universal improvised-weapon Mastery customization: rejected because it made random objects into optimized Fighter weapons rather than tools for Rogue precision.

- Heavy Intelligence dependency: rejected; the 'scientific' theme is represented through technique, not a mandatory secondary ability score.

- Acrobatics/Expertise-based grapple contests: rejected because 2024 Grapple/Shove uses saving throws rather than opposed skill checks, and Rogue Expertise would create unnecessary complications.

## 4.2 Cane Mastery Alternatives

- Topple: historically obvious because disturbing equilibrium is central to Bartitsu, but it overlaps heavily with the Rogue’s existing Trip Cunning Strike.

- Slow: thematically supports leg attacks and range control, but provides less direct synergy with the subclass’s counterattack loop.

- Sap: provisionally chosen because it weakens the target’s next attack and therefore supports Exploit an Opening when the target subsequently misses.

## 4.3 Reaction-Attack Balance Concern

An early design pass treated off-turn Sneak Attack as something that might need to be suppressed. That concern was later rejected. Sneak Attack is intentionally once per turn rather than once per round, and existing Rogue play already permits off-turn Sneak Attacks through Opportunity Attacks and other reaction-enabling mechanics. The subclass therefore keeps its reaction attacks fully compatible with Sneak Attack.

## 4.4 Alternate Level 9 Directions

- Initial version: level 9 merely added Disarm and Joint Lock as new Cunning Strike options.

- Revised version: level 9 instead specializes in the entire Cunning Strike system by reducing every Cunning Strike cost by 1d6 and also adds four new options.

- Joint Lock was initially considered at a 1d6 base cost. It was raised to 2d6 because it combines full attack damage with a Grapple rider.

- Redirect was considered as up to 10 feet of chosen-direction forced movement. The current provisional version uses 5 feet because directional repositioning is stronger than a straight push.

- Turnabout was considered as an alternative to Redirect, exchanging the Rogue’s and target’s spaces. It was not selected for the current version but remains a strong thematic alternative.

## 4.5 Alternate Level 13 and 17 Directions

- Level 13 was initially described broadly as advanced counterfighting or an Uncanny Dodge upgrade. It crystallized into Counterstroke: a hit answered by Uncanny Dodge can also be answered by an Advantage counterattack.

- Generic level-17 concepts such as 'complete adaptability', broad defensive mastery, or a standalone finishing maneuver were considered but not developed.

- The current capstone instead upgrades the subclass’s established Cunning Strike mastery: a 2d6 cost reduction and up to three different effects on one Sneak Attack.

# 5. Balance and Rules Notes

## 5.1 Damage Profile

The subclass intentionally increases the frequency of off-turn Sneak Attacks rather than adding Extra Attack or a second damage die progression. At level 13, Counterstroke can make the reaction attack available whether a nearby melee attacker hits or misses: a miss triggers Exploit an Opening, while a hit can trigger Uncanny Dodge plus Counterstroke. This is a substantial melee reward, but it requires the Rogue to remain within 5 feet, be personally attacked, retain the Reaction, hit the counterattack, and still satisfy Sneak Attack’s normal conditions.

## 5.2 Cunning Strike Economy

Scientific Leverage reduces each Cunning Strike cost by 1d6, to a minimum of 0d6. This makes the basic 1d6 options free at level 9 and allows two normally-1d6 options to be applied for free once Improved Cunning Strike arrives at level 11. Perfect Leverage increases the reduction to 2d6 at level 17 and permits three different Cunning Strike effects on one attack.

| **Cunning Strike**                           | **Normal Cost** | **Cost at Level 17** |
|----------------------------------------------|-----------------|----------------------|
| Poison / Trip / Withdraw / Disarm / Redirect | 1d6             | 0d6                  |
| Daze / Joint Lock / Suppress                 | 2d6             | 0d6                  |
| Obscure                                      | 3d6             | 1d6                  |
| Knock Out                                    | 6d6             | 4d6                  |

## 5.3 Signature Cane Scaling

The Cane’s +1/+2/+3 progression is treated as practitioner skill rather than the object becoming magical. This allows the signature weapon to remain competitive with expected magic-weapon accuracy without requiring a special enchanted item, and replacement canes inherit the benefit because the bonus belongs to the subclass feature.

# 6. Current Provisional Subclass

The following is the current working version. It is intentionally presented as a sourcebook-style option, but all names, numbers, and wording remain provisional.

**ROGUISH ARCHETYPE: BARTITSU**

Bartitsu practitioners approach combat as a problem of timing, leverage, and opportunity. They combine precise strikes, deceptive movement, joint manipulation, and an innocuous walking stick with the Rogue’s instinct for exploiting an opening.

A practitioner rarely meets force with force. Instead, they invite an opponent to overcommit, disturb their balance, and answer with a decisive strike or controlling technique. Their training also teaches them to remain dangerous when disarmed, turning their own body—or whatever happens to be at hand—into an effective weapon.

Bartitsu Training

*3rd-Level Bartitsu Feature*

Your training teaches you to fight through precision, misdirection, and whatever means are at hand.

You gain proficiency with Improvised Weapons. Your Unarmed Strikes and Improvised Weapons count as weapons with the Finesse property for you.

When you use the Grapple or Shove option of your Unarmed Strike, you can use Dexterity instead of Strength to determine the saving throw DC.

You also gain the following ways to create and exploit openings in an opponent’s defense.

**Create an Opening.** As a Bonus Action, choose one creature within 5 feet of you. You have Advantage on the next attack roll you make against that creature before the end of the current turn with your Cane, an Improvised Weapon, or a damaging Unarmed Strike.

**Exploit an Opening.** When a creature within 5 feet of you misses you with a melee attack, you can use your Reaction to make one attack against that creature with your Cane, an Improvised Weapon, or a damaging Unarmed Strike. You have Advantage on the attack roll.

Cane

*3rd-Level Bartitsu Feature*

A walking stick can be an ordinary accessory in one person’s hands and a formidable weapon in another’s.

You gain a Cane. It can take the form of a walking stick, umbrella, parasol, baton, or similar handheld object or accessory. Regardless of its appearance, it uses the following statistics.

| **Weapon** | **Damage**      | **Properties**  | **Mastery** |
|------------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Cane       | 1d6 Bludgeoning | Versatile (1d8) | Sap         |

You are proficient with your Cane, and it counts as an Improvised Weapon for you.

Your Cane can function normally as the mundane object or accessory it resembles. Its nature as a weapon isn’t apparent from its appearance alone.

When you finish a Short or Long Rest, you can designate a suitable object in your possession as your Cane. It thereafter uses the statistics above.

**Practiced Cane.** Your skill with your Cane improves as you gain Rogue levels. When you wield your Cane, you gain the following bonus to its attack and damage rolls:

| **Rogue Level** | **Bonus** |
|-----------------|-----------|
| 3–8             | —         |
| 9–12            | +1        |
| 13–16           | +2        |
| 17+             | +3        |

Scientific Leverage

*9th-Level Bartitsu Feature*

Your understanding of leverage, timing, balance, and anatomy allows you to employ disabling techniques without sacrificing as much of the force behind your attacks.

The die cost of each Cunning Strike effect you use is reduced by 1d6, to a minimum cost of 0d6.

In addition, the following effects are added to your Cunning Strike options.

**Disarm (Cost: 1d6).** The target must succeed on a Strength saving throw or drop one object of your choice that it is holding. The object lands in the target’s space.

**Redirect (Cost: 1d6).** If the target is no more than one size larger than you, it must succeed on a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (its choice) or you move it up to 5 feet to an unoccupied space you can see.

**Joint Lock (Cost: 2d6).** If the target is no more than one size larger than you and you have a hand free, it must succeed on a Strength or Dexterity saving throw (its choice) or have the Grappled condition, with you as the grappler.

**Suppress (Cost: 2d6).** The target must succeed on a Dexterity saving throw or be unable to take Reactions until the start of your next turn.

Counterstroke

*13th-Level Bartitsu Feature*

You have learned to turn even a successful attack into an opening.

When a creature within 5 feet of you hits you with a melee attack and you use Uncanny Dodge against that attack, you can make one attack against that creature as part of the same Reaction using your Cane, an Improvised Weapon, or a damaging Unarmed Strike. You have Advantage on the attack roll.

Perfect Leverage

*17th-Level Bartitsu Feature*

Your mastery of Bartitsu allows you to combine disabling techniques with almost no loss of offensive momentum.

The reduction to Cunning Strike costs provided by your Scientific Leverage increases from 1d6 to 2d6, to a minimum cost of 0d6.

In addition, when you use Improved Cunning Strike, you can apply up to three different Cunning Strike effects to the same attack instead of two. You must still pay the cost of each effect.

# 7. Open Questions for Future Passes

- Subclass name: “Bartitsu” is currently functional but may eventually be replaced with a setting-neutral D&D name while keeping Bartitsu as the design inspiration.

- Cane mastery: Sap is provisional and should be tested against Slow and Topple in actual play.

- Cane concealment ribbon: the current wording says its nature as a weapon is not apparent from appearance alone; this may need a defined Investigation/Perception interaction or may be better left narrative.

- Redirect wording: confirm whether 5 feet is sufficient and whether forced movement needs any terrain or size restrictions beyond the current one-size-larger limit.

- Suppress save: Dexterity is provisional; another save or no save may ultimately fit better depending on balance.

- Cunning Strike cost reduction: test free Trip/Withdraw/Poison at level 9 and free 2d6 options at level 17, especially when combined through Improved Cunning Strike.

- Counterstroke DPR: test encounters where the Rogue is attacked repeatedly in melee to ensure reliable off-turn Sneak Attack remains within the desired power target.

- Magic-item interaction: determine whether a magical cane or magic improvised object can be designated as the subclass Cane and how its existing bonuses/properties interact with Practiced Cane.

# 8. Design Status

All features in Section 6 are provisionally locked as the current working version. “Locked” here means they are the baseline for the next design pass, not that they are exempt from later wording, balance, or playtest revisions.
