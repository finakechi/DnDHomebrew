# **Bartitsu: Historical Overview and Design Foundations**

## **Origins and Context**  
Edward William Barton-Wright (1860–1951) was an English engineer turned martial arts pioneer.  After extensive travels (1870–1898) and engineering work in Asia, he settled in Japan (c.1895–98).  There he studied Japanese jujutsu (notably the Shinden Fudo-ryū style in Kobe and early Kodokan Jūdō in Tokyo), as well as maintaining his boxing skill.  Returning to London in 1898, Barton-Wright combined these Eastern arts with Western combat methods (boxing and savate kicking, plus a stick-fighting style taught by Swiss fencing master Pierre Vigny) to form a new self-defence system.  He coined the term **“Bartitsu”** (a portmanteau of “Barton-Wright” and “jiujitsu”).  He defined Bartitsu as **“self-defence in all its forms”**.

The Bartitsu School of Arms and Physical Culture was opened in Soho, London around 1900 (67b Shaftesbury Avenue).  The school offered instruction in jujitsu, boxing, Savate, the Vigny stick system, and other arts.  It operated like a Victorian athletic club – members applied and were vetted by a committee.  Notably, the school admitted women (teaching stick and jujitsu as women’s self-defence), which was unusual for the era.  Members included martial arts enthusiasts, suffragettes, even aristocrats. 

Victorian London was rife with crime and street violence, especially in impoverished districts like Whitechapel (Jack the Ripper murders, 1888).  Public concern over sudden, brutal attacks (often using weapons) was high.  Barton-Wright’s motivation was explicitly to **protect weaker or surprised people** from the “unexpected” attacks of criminals or drunkards who would use any means (chairs, bottles, knives) to win.  As he wrote, foreign assailants would use any available weapon, whereas Britons tended to fight “fairly” (with fists).  To counter this, Bartitsu was designed as an **eclectic, practical system**.  Barton-Wright emphasized principles over ritual: “The principle may be briefly summed up as follows: (1) disturb the equilibrium of your assailant; (2) surprise him before he regains balance; (3) if necessary, torque his joints beyond their natural limits.” These principles enable a smaller man to defeat a larger one by leverage, balance, and targeting joints.  

Bartitsu’s combination of Asian and European methods was highly unusual for 1900.  It has been called “the first combat system to combine Asian and European martial arts” – in effect, a proto-­mixed-martial-arts.  Barton-Wright staged challenge matches pitting his jujutsu and cane fighters against boxers, wrestlers, and other opponents, anticipating the MMA craze by nearly a century.  Unlike sport boxing or catch wrestling, Bartitsu was explicitly *pragmatic* self-defense.  It was not practiced as a competitive sport; its goal was always to **incapacitate or restrain** attackers quickly, not to score points or win a fair fight.  

The Bartitsu Club’s heyday was brief.  By around 1903 it had closed, for reasons that remain unclear (financial problems and Barton-Wright’s shifting interests were factors). Barton-Wright himself soon abandoned martial instruction for his physiotherapy clinics.  After World War I Bartitsu vanished from public memory.  Its only legacy was a misremembered mention in Arthur Conan Doyle’s 1903 story *“The Adventure of the Empty House”* – Holmes credited “baritsu” (sic) with saving his life.  The term intrigued Sherlock Holmes fans for decades, keeping a faint echo of Bartitsu alive.  Bartitsu was effectively forgotten until the early 2000s. 

**What made Bartitsu unusual** was its *eclectic breadth and scientific approach*.  It deliberately drew on multiple traditions (jujutsu, boxing, Savate, cane-fighting) and on anatomical and mechanical analysis.  Barton-Wright even authored articles on “poses” and strength feats.  He showed realistic photographs of techniques to illustrate leverage.  Rather than focusing on monastic discipline or Victorian gentlemanly combat, Bartitsu taught “getting out of trouble” by any means.  It was targeted at *civilian self-defense in urban settings*, not military swordplay or dueling.

Some modern accounts have called Bartitsu an early form of MMA.  This is partly true in that it mixed styles, but it is **misleading** to equate it directly with modern sports.  Bartitsu was non-competitive and embedded in a *physical culture* milieu (physical fitness, electro-therapy, regimen, etc.).  It also included Victorian-era attitudes (women’s self-defence, “gentlemanly” appearance with hidden skill).  In short, Bartitsu was an idiosyncratic hybrid fitting its time: a *self-defense curriculum* rather than a sport.

## **Component Arts in Bartitsu**

### **Japanese Jujitsu / Jujutsu**  
Barton-Wright’s core was Japanese jujutsu (often spelled “jiu-jitsu” then).  He studied “ko-ryū” (old-school) jujutsu in Japan, notably the *Shinden Fudō-ryū* style in Kobe.  From jujutsu Bartitsu inherited *throws, trips, joint locks, and grips*.  Techniques like hip throws, foot sweeps, and wrist or elbow locks (“subjecting joints to strains”) are evident in Barton-Wright’s instructions.  He showed how to topple or arm-lock bigger men by leverage rather than strength.  Stand-up grappling (grip fighting, breaking balance) and escapes from grabs would have been part of this.  Defenses against bear-hugs or chokes (using knee strikes, breaks) likely drew on jujutsu.  

Importantly, this era’s jujutsu was **not** like modern Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu.  Groundfighting was not a focus; throws and locks were applied standing, and many techniques expected an opponent to call “Mattanai!” (yield) when a lock was set.  Newaza (ground grappling) was minimal.  Thus Bartitsu jujitsu was about quickly seizing “structural” advantage, not wrestling for submissions on the floor.  (The Kodokan Judo Barton-Wright learned would also emphasize throws and pins, but he used the term “jiujitsu”.)

### **British Boxing (Pugilism)**  
Bartitsu incorporated **late‑Victorian boxing** techniques.  By 1900 British prizefighting was generally gloved (Queensberry Rules, at least in law) and featured upright stances.  A typical stance had the lead hand extended and the rear forearm protecting the body.  Important skills would have included straight punches (jab, cross) and hooks, parrying and slipping punches, and footwork (stepping in and out or pivoting).  Since Bartitsu was self-defense, boxing was used both offensively (to stun or damage an attacker) and defensively (to evade or close distance).  For example, a boxer’s footwork could be used to move into clinching or grappling range safely, or to escape kicks or holds.  The Victorian boxing ethos (e.g. fair play versus an armed assailant) may have been cast aside; Barton-Wright’s system was entirely practical, even if Victorian gentlemen often liked to think of pugilism as honorable.  

### **Savate (French Boxing/Kicking)**  
Savate contributed Bartitsu’s **kicking techniques**.  Late-19th-century savate (la canne aside) was evolving from bare-foot street-fighting kicks to the formal “French boxing” with shoes.  Key kicks likely included low, swift kicks aimed at an attacker’s shins, knees, or groin – techniques suitable for knocking down or unbalancing without requiring high flexibility.  High or fancy kicks were probably less emphasized; Barton-Wright’s illustrations show mostly low stances.  Savate’s footwork (evasive sideways steps, forward lunges) would also factor into range control.  (Note: modern competitive Savate includes many high kicks, but the Bartitsu version was simpler.)  The main use of Savate in Bartitsu would be to destabilize an opponent’s footing or create openings – for example, a well-placed calf or front kick to enable a follow-up throw or punch.  

### **Pierre Vigny’s Walking-Stick Method**  
One of Bartitsu’s hallmark elements is **stick (cane) fighting** from Pierre Vigny’s method.  Barton-Wright explicitly integrated Vigny’s cane-fighting system into Bartitsu.  Vigny was a Swiss master-at-arms who taught a combative form of French la canne.  His “walking-stick method” treated the cane not like a fencing sword but as an independent weapon suited for self-defense.  Barton-Wright described this new cane art as **“essentially different from single-stick or sword-play”**. 

Key features of Vigny’s cane work: The stick is gripped with the thumb overlapping the fingers (a full fist grip), and swung using the wrist and body rather than blade techniques. Guards and parries emphasize deflecting blows off the stick to protect the hands.  Vigny’s preferred stance is a *“high guard”* (the stick held above the head at one end, pointing at the opponent’s eyes).  From that guard, short, cutting strikes can be delivered with wrist action.  Both ends of the cane are used: the heavy metal-tip end delivers powerful strikes, and the protruding butt-end can function like a short dagger (e.g. jabbing at exposed ribs).  

Tactically, Vigny’s cane system keeps distance and leverages the stick’s reach.  Defensively, one “guard by distance” by pivoting the feet rather than crossing arms.  Offensively, typical strikes target the head, clavicle, ribs, limbs, and knees.  Counter-attacks often exploit an opponent’s commitment: Vigny drills include baiting an attack and then “swinging the body” to deliver a swift retaliatory cut to the attacker’s exposed wrist, temple, or limbs.  The cane can be used two-handed when heavier, with grips allowing the defender to ambiguously prepare an overhand cut (revealing neither end is obvious).  

Barton-Wright’s own writings emphasize that **with a stout Malacca cane one can sever a man’s jugular through his coat** if used properly – underscoring the cane’s lethality in skilled hands.  He also notes that a cane must be used so that blows “slide away from the hand instead of toward it”, to avoid disarmament.  

### **Other Influences**  
The Bartitsu Club also offered other martial arts, though Barton-Wright did *not* necessarily include them in “Bartitsu technique” itself.  Notably:

- **Wrestling:** Swiss wrestler Armand Cherpillod taught wrestling and general physical culture.  He likely instructed in catch-as-catch-can style grappling (common European wrestling of the day).  This added to the curriculum but may have overlapped with or reinforced the jujitsu teachings.  The extent to which Bartitsu integrated pure wrestling moves is unclear, but wresting in the club would emphasize holds and throws not covered by jujitsu.  

- **Fencing (Historical and Modern):** Captain Alfred Hutton taught historical fencing (rapier, dagger, Elizabethan swordplay) at the school.  Hutton was influential in European martial-arts revival, but Barton-Wright regarded fencing as separate from his self-defence art.  Hutton himself studied Vigny cane and jujitsu while at Bartitsu, but fencing techniques (e.g. ripostes, thrusts with a sword) were *not* listed among Bartitsu’s core method.  Thus, fencing skills were taught to members but not counted as Bartitsu techniques.  

- **Kabukite (Jutte) or other small weapons:** There is little evidence Bartitsu included such weapons formally.  However, the principle “anything that comes to hand” suggests improvised weapons (umbrella, top hat) were encouraged.  Vigny’s articles even mention using a cane as an “ordinary carried object.”  

In summary, **integrated vs incidental**: Boxing, jujitsu, Savate, and Vigny stick-fighting were the canonical Bartitsu core (often called **“Canonical Bartitsu”** today).  Other arts (fencing, wrestling, gymnastics, even early electric therapy) were present at the club but generally considered supplementary.  Barton-Wright’s articles and the Bartitsu Society’s research distinguish “canon” versus “club offerings.”  We will use only the well-documented core techniques in our analysis, noting when something is speculative or derived from later “Neo-Bartitsu.”  

## **Bartitsu as an Integrated System**

Bartitsu was not taught as a mere list of unrelated techniques, but as an integrated **system of ranges and transitions**.  Barton-Wright wrote that the art “embraces every possible eventuality”, meaning one must fluidly move through different ranges and methods depending on the situation. 

### **Range Management:**  
- **Long range (stick/weapon distance):** In cane range, the practitioner uses the cane as primary reach weapon.  Vigny’s “guard by distance” stance (cane up high, lead foot forward, knees bent) keeps the enemy at a standoff range while inviting an overcommitment.  The cane strikes from here aim to disable or off-balance (e.g. chopping leg or shoulder).  

- **Mid range (kicking range):** At this distance, Bartitsu relies on Savate kicks and long punches.  The fighter can pivot in to deliver a Savate low kick (shin/knee) to upset balance, then retreat or step in.  Dixon into kicking range is also an opportunity to spring into boxing range if needed.  

- **Punching range:** Here western boxing skills dominate.  The user employs straight punches, feints, parries, and footwork.  A boxer’s quick jab or hook can stun the attacker or create a line for a follow-up (e.g. stepping in to grab).  For example, Barton-Wright claimed Bartitsu can handle “attacks by a boxer” as well as by knifemen, implying versatile transition from defending strikes to grappling.  

- **Close combat/grappling range:** When in clinch or grapple distance, jujitsu techniques take over.  One might execute throws, trips, or locks.  For example, a Bartitsu fighter may slip a punch and catch the arm, immediately applying a hip throw (leveraging strength with technique).  Joint locks (e.g. wrenches on a wrist or shoulder) would force an attacker to break off.  If the opponent pushes or holds, one can employ takedowns.  From ground-brawler era photos, Barton-Wright emphasized that a small man “once the opponent is at his mercy he will cry ‘Hold!’”.  

A practitioner must be prepared to **chain** actions: e.g. parry a cane attack (pivot out of range) → immediately thrust with cane or strike → if the opponent draws close, drop the cane or switch to two-handed grappling to throw.  The notion is constantly adjusting **distance**: if under threat at close range, create space; if opponent is beyond reach, close swiftly.  For instance, using “Withdraw” tactics (a new Rogue feature) analogizes the idea of striking then slipping back (exactly what Barton-Wright’s “guard by distance” does).  

### **Tactical Principles:**  
A survey of Barton-Wright’s writing and Bartitsu lore reveals recurring tactical themes:

- **Control the initiative:** Bartitsu students were taught to strike or act “before [the assailant] has time to use his strength”.  That may mean preemptively grabbing, striking, or off-balancing an opponent who seems about to attack.  In D&D terms, this suggests ambush or disrupting actions (Sneak Attack fits this well).  

- **Maintain/change distance:** The system includes both tactics to **retreat** (back-pivot of guard) and to **advance** (sudden step into closing distance).  Moves like Bartitsu’s “Withdraw” (which lets the Rogue disengage after an attack) literally emulate these.  Controlling distance also means never allowing a stronger opponent to comfortably utilize his strength (e.g. not getting thrown by him).  

- **Use offense for defense:** A major motto is that the best defense can be attack – Barton-Wright’s title is “self-defence”, yet his instructions emphasize counterattacks.  Cane parries immediately send a counter cut; a deflected punch is often turned into a lock or break.  Even feints can be considered defensive: feinting an arm exposes an opening which is the trap the defender sets.  

- **Exploit openings and commitment:** If an opponent overcommits (e.g. throws a punch or lunge), the Bartitsu practitioner capitalizes by unbalancing him.  For example, dropping the left arm and turning triggers an overreach by the attacker, who then gets clubbed on the wrist as his balance is off.  In design terms, this suggests many effects “trigger on enemy attack” or force enemies to lose balance.  

- **Disrupt equilibrium:** Central to Bartitsu is unbalancing the foe.  All cane and grappling techniques aim at this.  In game terms, this is “knocking prone” (Topple property), or imposing movement penalties (Slow property), or forcing repositioning.  Nailing an opponent’s legs or knees would reduce their movement.  

- **Leverage over strength:** Barton-Wright repeatedly notes that the art allows a weak man to overcome a strong one by leverage.  For Rogues, this suggests relying on Dexterity and precision rather than Strength.  Abilities like “Topple” on a failed save embody that principle: even a weak blow can topple a strong foe if caught off-balance.  

- **Joint manipulation:** Bartitsu literature mentions attacks on any joint (wrist, elbow, shoulder, etc.).  In practice this could become grappling “maneuvers” that impose penalties or conditions.  In D&D terms, effects like disarming (targeting wrists), immobilizing (grappling), or forcing saves (where failing represents a joint lock) make sense.  

- **Adaptability:** “Not seeking a quarrel… defence and counter must be entirely based upon the tactics of your opponent”.  The art has no fixed sequences; students learned many techniques so they could mix-and-match.  Mechanically, this suggests flexibility: the subclass should not force a single “special attack” but a toolbox triggered by circumstances.  

In summary, **historical evidence → practical meaning → possible design theme**: We will use citations from Barton-Wright and period sources to identify each principle (e.g. “disturb equilibrium”) then explicate how that might translate into gameplay concepts (e.g. Knocking Prone, forced movement, attack reactions).  Where direct evidence is lacking, we will classify the idea’s support (canonical vs. plausible vs. neo-Bartitsu) later. 

## **Canonical Techniques (by Function)**

While not exhaustive, we can illustrate representative Bartitsu maneuvers organized by function.  (These are *conceptual sketches*, not full instruction.)  Sources include Barton-Wright’s articles and later reconstructions:

- **Maintain Distance:** *Guard by Distance* (Vigny): Adopt the rear-guard stance (cane high, lead foot forward) and pivot away from each incoming attack, keeping the opponent just at reach.  *Game concept:* Automatically step back (disengage) when an enemy tries to enter melee, possibly imposing disadvantage on their attack roll.

- **Close Distance:** A lunge-punch or kick to press forward, followed by a leg sweep.  For example, step in with a boxing jab, then hook the opponent’s leg to trip.  *Game concept:* Combine a melee attack with a built-in Shove/Trip attempt (like Battlemaster Trip) without expending a separate action.  

- **Break Distance:** A strong backward step or sidestep (darting out of range), using Withdraw (Dex save to avoid OA) or a Dodge action to avoid retaliation.  

- **Counter a Punch:** Slip the punch (roll under it) and counter with a straight elbow strike or cane butt-stroke to a joint. *Game:* On successful Disengage or Dodge, gain a free Unarmed Strike (with Dex) that imposes a status (e.g. restrained on target if the attack hits, representing an arm lock).

- **Counter a Kick:** Parry the leg (using cane or block), then immediately execute a hip throw.  *Game:* When hit by a melee attack while wielding a “Bartitsu weapon” (cane/club), use a Reaction to grab & throw (target Dex save or fall prone).

- **Counter a Grab:** If grabbed by the arm, perform a wrist-lock that forces the foe to their knees. *Game:* Once per turn, when grappled or restrained, a save (Dex or Str) against the Rogue’s Athletics (or Cunning) check leads to the attacker being Shoveled or becoming restrained (roleplay as joint manipulation).

- **Counter a Weapon:** If someone lunges with a knife, intercept the blade with the cane tip, twist it aside. *Game:* A Reaction “Deflect Miss” that imposes disadvantage on one weapon attack or negates it, with a chance to disarm (Con save for the attacker to keep the weapon).

- **Off-Balance:** A strong raking kick to the knee. *Game:* Sneak Attack option “Topple” (con save or prone) akin to quarterstaff mastery.  

- **Trip:** A sweeping cane hook behind the attacker’s ankle, making them fall. *Game:* Trip (with advantage if you hid intent, representing feint).

- **Throw:** A jujutsu hip throw (koshi-guruma) after grabbing an arm. *Game:* If you hit with Sneak Attack, you may spend extra damage die to instead force the target to make a Dex save or be knocked prone.

- **Restrain/Control:** A two-handed cane choke (pressing the butt end under the chin and locking hands). *Game:* Grappling maneuver with both hands and weapon; treat as Grapple with damage.

- **Escape:** From a bear-hug: drop weight (center yourself) and step out, twisting the hold. *Game:* Bonus action to escape grapples even when normally blocked (Dex save to slip out).  

- **Strike (fist):** Straight punch to the solar plexus. *Game:* A Dexterity-based unarmed attack (if allowed) that deals 1d4+Dex and counts for Sneak Attack.

- **Strike (cane):** Overhead cane chop to the head. *Game:* Unarmed or club strike with Topple effect.  

- **Kick:** Low front kick to the knee cap. *Game:* Unarmed strike forcing target to make Str save or have speed reduced (like Push/Slow).

- **Cane Defense:** Using the butt end near your hand to stop blade. *Game:* Reaction: add proficiency bonus to AC versus one melee attack if holding cane.  

- **Disarm:** A hooking sweep of the wrist with cane. *Game:* On a hit with Sneak Attack, target must save or drop held item (like Battlemaster Disarm).

- **Manipulate a Joint:** Wrench a raised arm downward (shoulder lock). *Game:* On hit, target makes Str save or becomes restrained.

- **Use Terrain:** Press attacker into wall, then pop knee to stomach. *Game:* Pushing a target into an obstacle grants extra damage or prone.

- **Create Opening:** Feint with cane, drawing defender’s parry, then strike elsewhere. *Game:* A bonus action "Feint Attack" granting advantage on next attack (already exists in Battlemaster, could analogize).

Each of these draws on Bartitsu’s corpus (Bart’s or Vigny’s manuals) and the principle of turning defense into offense.  We must be careful never to present these as exact historical “moves,” but as examples of tactics Barton-Wright described (or that modern Bartitsu reconstructors use).  

## **Canonical vs. Neo-Bartitsu**  

*Canonical Bartitsu* (well-documented) consists of the techniques and tactics Barton-Wright himself published between 1899–1902.  This includes the Pearson’s Magazine articles and the Bartitsu School’s curricula (jujitsu sequences, cane guard, etc.).  The Bartitsu Society has collected these in their *Bartitsu Compendium*.  We have high confidence in the core principles listed above, as they’re explicitly from Barton-Wright’s writings.  

*Lost information:* We cannot know exactly how certain throws or locks were taught (the Pearson articles lacked detail).  For example, the specifics of many grappling techniques, or foot sweeps, rely on general jujutsu knowledge.  These would be **Plausible Reconstructions** based on similar arts (Shinden Fudo, Kodokan of that era).  Similarly, we lack details on subtle footwork and counters – we must infer from context.  

*Neo-Bartitsu* refers to the modern revival starting around 2002.  It uses canonical Bartitsu plus later early-20th-century martial practices that Barton-Wright’s students might have known (like small-guns disarm, hand-to-hand tactics from 1910s self-defense books).  We should mark Neo-Bartitsu additions as **Modern Extension**: for example, any notion of “using an umbrella as a sword” (common in Holmes fiction) isn’t in Barton-Wright’s time – that is Sherlock-inspired.  Likewise, modern fetishization of knuckle push-ups or extreme kicks would be Neo.

In summary, we will tag our historical conclusions with confidence:
- **Well Documented:** Found in Barton-Wright’s own text or contemporary accounts. E.g. cane guards, principle of imbalance.
- **Strongly Supported:** Corroborated by multiple period sources (Pearson’s, Bartitsu Society research). E.g. inclusion of British boxing, existence of Japanese instructors.
- **Plausible Reconstruction:** Common martial rationale fitting the era (e.g. Dex grappling, general wrestling throws).
- **Modern Extension:** Interpretations with little direct evidence (e.g. fancy cinematic moves, some Holmes-derived ideas).

These distinctions will guide how boldly we propose game mechanics from each concept.  

## **The Bartitsu Character Fantasy**  

A Bartitsu practitioner in fiction is typically a **cunning self-defense specialist**.  Key flavor themes:  
- **Adaptable Warrior:** Seamlessly shifts among cane, fists, kicks, grapples. Not reliant on a single style.  
- **Intellectual Fighter:** Fights scientifically – reads an opponent’s movements, exploits biomechanics. (Hence the “scientific” label of Barton-Wright’s writing.)  
- **Gentleman’s Self-Defense:** Outwardly civilized, wearing a top hat and cane, yet deadly if provoked. (Bart’s Victorian audience were gentlemen concerned with propriety.)  
- **Parry & Riposte Expert:** Uses an opponent’s attack as moment to counter (echoes of Holmes turning “baritsu” move on Col. Moran).  
- **Prepared and Resourceful:** Carrying a cane as a tool, training for any eventuality, always mindful of environment (on bench or in alley).  
- **Physical, not Mystical:** Not channeling ki or magic – just skill and ingenuity.  

We must separate *Sherlock-Holmes pop culture* from history.  Doyle’s Holmes story is the origin of the public myth.  Holmes is said to have “practiced baritsu” to kill Professor Moriarty.  This led to many Holmes pastiches where he uses “baritsu” as a flashy move (sometimes handwaves, sometimes cane tricks, sometimes a Uryu technique in manga).  Those **Holmesian tropes** (e.g. dramatic jumps, shooting a man in the exact right anatomical weakness with a cane) are largely fictional.  We should use Sherlock imagery only if they match true Bartitsu flavor – for example, Holmes’s use of the cane for defense is Holmes fiction, but it aligns with Vigny’s system, so it’s fine as inspiration but not treated as historical fact.  

Bartitsu fantasy also connects to the Edwardian spy/investigator archetype.  Think of a detective or spy who trained in a gymnasium of combat arts.  Tying into Roguish “Investigator” skills (Insight, Perception) is plausible: Barton-Wright himself was very observant about opponents’ tactics.  History does mention Mrs. Barton-Wright as being severe about correct technique, implying attentiveness.  So reading opponents’ eyes and body language can be a justified subclass theme (though this is more the D&D fiction than 1900 tradition).  

In short, Bartitsu fiction can flavor: **steel-faced calm under attack, turning a threat back on the foe, using everyday items as weapons**.  But avoid granting supernatural “baritsu powers” or suggesting every Bartitsu practitioner is an aristocrat.  The core fantasy is *clever, well-prepared fighter who knows many arts and uses them opportunistically*.  

## **Bartitsu and the Rogue Class**  

Bartitsu’s blend of cunning, precision, and opportunism maps well onto the Rogue archetype.  Like Rogues, Bartitsu fighters target an opponent’s vulnerabilities rather than brawling powerfully.  They avoid straight strength contests, instead using timing and technique – much as Rogues avoid direct Strength clashes (e.g. grappling) in favor of Dexterity and skill.  Sneak Attack represents landing that one clever disabling blow, very in line with Bartitsu’s strike-then-withdraw approach.  

Compare to other classes:  
- *Monk:* Also agile and unarmed, but monks in D&D are often mystical and use ki or special defenses (unarmored AC, magical strikes).  Bartitsu has none of that.  A Bartitsu Rogue should not get ki points or large numbers of bonus unarmed strikes per turn – that veers into “kung fu”.  Instead, Bartitsu should leverage Rogue features (Sneak Attack, Cunning Action) for its style.  
- *Fighter/Battlemaster:* Bartitsu has elements of the Battlemaster (trip, disarm maneuvers), but fighters excel at sustained damage and heavy armor.  Bartitsu falls short of raw combat endurance and shield-blocking.  It’s more about one-on-one quick fights in civilian clothes, not battlefield tactics.  Moreover, making it a Fighter subclass might overshadow Rogue.  
- *Battle Master (or maneuver-using Fighter):* One might think Bartitsu is like Battle Master maneuvers (disarm, trip, parry, etc.).  Indeed, Cunning Strike acts like a limited set of maneuvers.  The difference is *resource use* – Battle Master uses superiority dice; Rogue uses Sneak Attack dice opportunistically.  From a game perspective, it’s likely cleaner to tie Bartitsu maneuvers into **Cunning Strike** (which is Sneak Attack’s add-on) than create a separate pool of “Bartitsu points”.  

Why especially Rogue: Rogues value *surprise and exploitation*.  Their basic flavor is one strike at the right moment.  Bartitsu is fundamentally opportunistic: waiting for or creating an opening (distraction, imbalance) and striking precisely.  2024 Rogue features like **Cunning Strike** (trading Sneak Attack dice for effects) and **Steady Aim** (gaining advantage for that one hit) align perfectly with the Bartitsu ethos.  Also, Rogues are skill experts – Bartitsu requires knowledge of anatomy (Medicine/Insight?), observation (Perception/Investigation), and agility (Acrobatics).  Many Bartitsu moves respond to social cues (a drunk lunge, a hidden weapon), so Insight or Investigation fits the detective vibe.  

Mechanically, Bartitsu should avoid overt Monkness: no unarmored defense (Rogues don’t get that), no Ki or Flurry of Blows (that gives many attacks per turn), no supernatural magic.  It should stay grounded: e.g. Dexterity-based attacks and stealth, not Wisdom-based mystical defense.  The subclass should not gain Extra Attack (Monk/Barbarian) or scale weapon damage (monk martial arts) that would overshadow the Rogue’s typical damage output.  Instead, it should *enhance and diversify* the Rogue’s existing options.  

Finally, Bartitsu themes fit Rogue abilities:
- *Opportunism & Openings:* Sneak Attack and Cunning Strike naturally exploit openings and response.
- *Mobility:* Cunning Action (Disengage/Hide) echoes Bartitsu’s evasive footwork.
- *Reactions:* Rogues have Uncanny Dodge; a Bartitsu rogue might get extra Reaction options (parry with cane, counter after a dodge).
- *Skill mastery:* Expertise in relevant skills (Athletics, Acrobatics, Insight, Perception) reflects the versatile, observant fighter.  

In summary, **a Bartitsu Rogue** should feel like *“a cunning technical fighter who turns an opponent’s moves against them”*, not like “a mystical martial artist” or “a brute.”  

## **2024 Rogue Mechanics (Baseline)**  

Under the 2024 revision, the Rogue class has some key features (source: D&D Beyond and official playtest).  Of special relevance:  

- **Sneak Attack:** Largely unchanged from earlier editions.  Requires a finesse or ranged weapon (or unarmed if rules allow).  It scales by +1d6 at Rog 1, then +1d6 every two levels, reaching 10d6 at level 19.  Sneak Attack can only be used once per turn if attack qualifies (an ally or advantage condition present).  

- **Weapon Mastery (Lvl 1):** Each day choose two weapons (proficient with) to gain their “mastery property”.  For example, mastery of the quarterstaff grants the *Topple* property: on a hit, target must make a Con save or fall prone.  Mastery of a club gives *Slow* (hit reduces target’s speed by 10 ft).  We will consider how the cane should be represented (see Part IX).  

- **Steady Aim (Lvl 3):** You can use a bonus action to gain advantage on one attack to enable Sneak Attack.  This saves the Rogue from needing an ally or hiding to get the special damage.  

- **Cunning Strike (Lvl 5):** When you deal Sneak Attack damage, you may forgo one or more dice to apply a special effect.  Initial options:  
  - *Poison (1d6):* Target fails Con save or becomes poisoned.  
  - *Trip (1d6):* Dex save or fall prone.  
  - *Withdraw (1d6):* Move up to half your speed without provoking OA.  
  These use a DC = 8 + Dex mod + prof (scales with Rogue).  

- **Improved Cunning Strike (Lvl 11):** Can apply *two* Cunning Strike effects to a single Sneak Attack (at cost of more dice).  

- **Devious Strikes (Lvl 14):** Adds new Cunning Strike options, notably *Daze* (Con save or target’s actions limited) and *Knock Out* (Con save or unconscious), and *Obscure* (Dex save or blinded).  These cost more dice (2d6+).  

- **Other Class Features:** Uncanny Dodge (halves one attack’s damage), Evasion, Reliable Talent, etc., function as normal (no direct Bartitsu tie-in).  Slippery Mind at 15 now gives proficiency in Wis and Cha saves.

**Subclass progression:** The Rogue’s subclass (“archetype”) features appear at levels 3, 9, 13, and 17.  The exact powers vary by archetype (Assassin, Thief, Trickster, Soulknife in 2024).  A new Bartitsu Rogue would slot its unique abilities into those levels.  For balance, it should be comparable to official subclasses: Thief gets bonus action item use and stealthy attack; Assassin gets assassination triggers; all typically grant steady but not overwhelming power.  

**Power budget:** Official Rogue subclasses do not greatly increase raw damage beyond Sneak Attack (except Assassin’s assured crits).  They add mobility, utility, or control.  For example, at low level a Thief’s Fast Hands & second-story work give extra actions for exploration; Assassin’s early Death Strike adds guaranteed crit vs surprised foes.  By mid levels, Assassin can do lots of damage on first round; Trickster has mage hand tweaks; Soulknife adds Psychic Blades and concealment.  By late levels, none add extra attacks per round, but they give situational control (free Standing Attack, etc.).  Our Bartitsu Rogue should similarly trade a little damage for strong tactical or defensive options.  

In our design context: we must keep Bartitsu’s features within the Rogue’s *existing framework*.  That likely means **Cunning Strike** is central (it’s explicitly designed for adding special effects).  It also means leaning on **Reactions** (since Bartitsu is reactive) without making a glut of them.  And using **Skill/Expertise** for flavor (investigation, perception, acrobatics).  We should avoid systems like new point pools or unbounded extra attacks.  

We have the rules context needed.  In what follows, we will refer back to these mechanics to see how Bartitsu concepts might fit – e.g., mapping “trip” to the Trip Cunning Strike, or using Weapon Mastery Topple for cane strikes, or adjusting Cunning Action for Bartitsu mobility.

## **The Cane in 2024 Rules**  

The walking-stick is Bartitsu’s icon.  Under 2024 rules, a normal cane would be a *simple weapon*, akin to a club (1d4 bludgeoning, light) or a quarterstaff (1d6/1d8 versatile) by stats.  Rogues are proficient with both (simple weapons).  However, neither has the **Finesse** property, so they normally **cannot** trigger Sneak Attack.  We must consider how to cleanly allow a Bartitsu rogue to use a cane effectively:

- **Option 1: Treat Cane as a Finesse Weapon.**  Perhaps a class feature declares “when you are wielding a cane, you can use your Dexterity instead of Strength for attack and damage rolls.”  This effectively makes it finesse.  Then it qualifies for Sneak Attack.  Balance: This gives the rogue a simple way to Sneak Attack with an innocuous weapon.  But note, rogues already have other finesse options (rapier, shortsword, etc.).  Allowing cane Sneak would mainly extend style/flavor.  

- **Option 2: Use Weapon Mastery.**  If we consider the cane a quarterstaff, the Rogue’s quarterstaff mastery property is *Topple* (forced prone on Con fail).  That is very thematic (knock them down).  If cane is a club, mastery is *Slow* (speed -10), also useful.  We could allow the rogue to select “quarterstaff” as one of their mastery weapons and use that for cane fights.  But without finesse, you’d have to rely on Sneak Attack from perhaps a dagger strike (less ideal for cane fantasy).  

- **Option 3: Special Sneak Attack Allowance.**  As an alternate design, one could give the subclass a feature: “Your sneak attack works with cane strikes.”  This might be accomplished by wording like “the cane is treated as a finesse weapon for you.”  

Considering flexibility and balance: **I recommend allowing the cane to be used as a finesse weapon by the Bartitsu Rogue**.  This can be phrased simply so as not to depend on mastery: e.g. “When wielding a cane (club or quarterstaff), you may use your Dexterity for the attack and damage.”  This matches the fantasy (use cane with nimble technique).  We also ensure the rogue can still use Sneak Attack with it.  

In game-world terms, a “Bartitsu walking stick” would effectively be a mundane quarterstaff (malacca cane) that any Rogue can pick up, so no exotic gear needed.  If needed for broader settings: it could be framed as “any sturdy club or walking stick” or even “any one-handed staff-like weapon”.  

Balance-wise, this is a moderate benefit (comparable to the Monk’s DEX option).  It should be integrated carefully (perhaps at level 3 or 5).  The subclass should also not force always carrying a cane; the feature might say “while wielding a simple club or quarterstaff, you treat it as finesse.”  That allows others weapons too.  

Finally, the **fantasy equivalent in other settings**: If a DM doesn’t want “Victorian cane” explicitly, we could say “combat staff” or even “spear (with special training)”.  But for design clarity, we’ll stick to “club/quarterstaff as finesse”.  

We’ll revisit the cane after addressing grappling.  

## **Unarmed Strikes and Grappling**  

Bartitsu also includes punching, kicking, throws, trips and holds.  D&D 2024’s revised *Unarmed Strike* allows grapples and shoves as variants, but by default it uses Strength for attack rolls and damage.  Rogues, however, typically dump Strength.  To capture Bartitsu’s use of agility in grappling, we consider letting the Rogue use Dexterity for certain maneuvers.  

Possibilities:  
- **Dexterous Unarmed Strike:** A feature that lets the rogue use Dex instead of Str for unarmed attacks (like Monk’s Martial Arts).  This would let them hit normally with kicks/punches using Dex, and qualify those attacks for Sneak (with the usual finesse caveat – note *Unarmed Strike* currently has no Finesse tag, so a further specification may be needed).  Balance risk: This edges toward Monk territory.  But without Flurry of Blows or unarmored defense, it might be acceptable.  

- **Dex Grappling:** Alternatively, allow using Acrobatics (Dex) instead of Athletics (Str) for Grapple/Shove contests.  For example, a Roguish move “Catch Off-Balance” could say: when you grapple or shove, you may use Acrobatics and Dex-based DC instead of Athletics (like the Monk’s optional rule).  This is potentially very strong with Expertise (Adv on those checks).  That deserves caution.  

- **Cunning Strike for Grapple:** Perhaps the class should rely on Cunning Strike for control (e.g. the Trip and Withdraw options).  The game already has disarm/knock prone etc. The subclass could *avoid* giving raw grapple superiority.  

Given that the Rogue is not meant to replace the Monk in grappling, I lean towards *limited Dex grappling*.  Perhaps: “When you grapple or shove, you may choose to use your Dexterity instead of Strength for the opposed check or for the DC.”  This would be a subclass feature.  Or simply treating grapple as an Unarmed Strike effect (though RAW grapples aren’t attacks).  If we allow Dex grapples, we must guard vs abuse (since Expertise + Reliable Talent + prof * might make it trivial to restrain small creatures).  Possibly require spending Sneak dice or a Cunning Strike for a grapple attempt.  

For now, we note: modern Bartitsu emphasis on leverage suggests you shouldn’t need brute Str to grapple effectively – a flagship design question (see *Open Questions* below).  

In any case, making unarmed attacks and grappling more Dexterous is thematically justifiable as *Plausible Reconstruction*.  

## **Cunning Strike as Bartitsu Core**  

Cunning Strike is a perfect mechanical fit for Bartitsu’s maneuvers.  Many Bartitsu tactics align with existing Cunning Strike options:  
- **Trip:** Exactly matches a Bartitsu trip or low sweep.  (Bartitsu cane or foot sweeps often knock an attacker prone.)  Trip (cost 1d6) is already a Rogue option.  

- **Disarm:** Not in base Cunning Strike, but could be a custom option.  Historically, Bartitsu did emphasize twist-disarms (e.g. twisting a hand open on a wrist lock, or striking the holding arm).  A “Disarm” cunning option (con save or drop held item) would be on-theme.  We must consider if that fits in *Devious Strikes* or earlier.  

- **Withdraw/Evasion:** The *Withdraw* option reflects Bartitsu’s “step out after attack” (foot pivoting).  It is explicitly in Cunning Strike.  This captures the idea of hit-and-run self-defense.  

- **Counter-attack (Reflexive):** Bartitsu often deals with opponent’s missed/failed attacks by countering.  For example, catching a failed strike and stabbing their exposed side.  Could Cunning Strike cover this?  Not directly, since it triggers on *your* Sneak Attack, not on enemy attack.  We might consider adding a Reaction: “If an enemy misses you with a melee attack, you can spend a Sneak Attack die to immediately strike back.”  That would be extra strong (double-turn usage).  It might overlap with *Uncanny Dodge* and normal Attacks of Opportunity.  Probably better to *not* give too many Reaction strikes; maybe a subclass *feature* at level 9 or 13: “Counter Strike – when a foe you can see misses you, you can make one unarmed strike or cane attack against it as a reaction.”  This fits theme (one for one).  But must check power creep: a rogue could then Sneak+counter in same round if timed well.  This is a critical balance check.

- **Parry/Defense:** Another common desire is a defensive reaction.  The classic “Parry” battlemaster maneuver (reduce damage) could be applied with a cane or deft motion.  Possibly using Uncanny Dodge covers this already (halving damage).  Maybe the subclass could extend Uncanny Dodge to add some effect (like halving and possibly allow a free Disengage).  However, too many reactions compete with each other.  

- **Creative Cunning Strikes:** Devious Strikes options (Knock Out, Daze, Obscure) add control/disable effects.  Bartitsu places emphasis on *ending fights quickly and quietly* – unconsciousing an assailant (Knock Out) is a bit extreme but flavor-wise fits a kill-shot to the temple.  Blinding (Obscure) could reflect hitting eyes or using cane tip as flash.  Daze (limiting actions) could be a quick confusion blow.  These work well as level 14 upgrades.  

Given this alignment, I propose:  
  - **Include Trip, Withdraw (already in).**  
  - **Add a Disarm option** (perhaps cost 2d6) to Cunning Strike, likely at Devious Strikes or as a subclass-specific option.  This could be justified by both historical cane use (striking wrists/elbows) and jujitsu (wrist locks).  
  - **Parry option** might conflict with Dodge/OA rules; perhaps skip or incorporate by Uncanny Dodge and existing Rogue reactions.  
  - **Counters:** If not by Cunning Strike, maybe a Reaction feature (see above).  
  - **Focus on integration:** We should avoid creating an entirely separate “grappling attack system.”  Instead, use existing mechanics: e.g. to trip someone, use the Trip Cunning Strike; to push, maybe use a Cane mastery *Push* (if club); to grab, use Grapple rules; to disarm, use our new option.  

We will carefully prioritize staying within Rogue’s tools (Cunning Action, Sneak/Cunning Strikes, reactions) rather than building new pools.  

## **Reactions and Defense**  

Bartitsu masters were taught to react to opponents’ moves: parry, counter, redirect.  In 5e/5.5, reactions include *Opportunity Attacks* and *Uncanny Dodge*.  We should consider subclass reactions:  

- **Parry with Cane (Reaction):** Possibly as a Cunning Strike-like exchange but as Reaction: “When a melee attack targets you, you can use your Reaction to impose disadvantage on that attack, if you are wielding a cane.”  This is effectively a 1/day or so ability unless costed.  But it sounds a lot like Uncanny Dodge (only half damage) plus disadvantage.  That might be overpowered.  If we do it, maybe replace Uncanny Dodge or limited uses.  

- **Counterattack (Reaction):** As mentioned, allowing a free attack when the enemy misses.  This could allow extra Sneak opportunities.  It might be best tuned as: “When a creature misses you with an attack, you can use your Reaction and deal an Unarmed Strike (using Dex) or cane attack against it.”  If allowed to Sneak Attack as part of that (if conditions met), it could significantly boost DPR.  But maybe restrict that Sneak Attack can only be done once per turn anyway.  

- **Evade/Slip (Reaction):** Possibly as a reaction after being hit (like Rogue’s Slippery Mind, but on fail vs an attack).  However, Uncanny Dodge already halves.  Maybe some subclass feature: “If you have your cane drawn, you may use a Reaction to impose disadvantage on one ranged attack targeting you (representing intercepting a thrown weapon with your stick)”.  This is very niche.  

Given Rogue already has one strong Reaction (Uncanny Dodge every round), adding more reactions can cause bookkeeping issues and overshadow Assassin’s auto-crits.  We should be judicious.  One idea: we could allow the use of Cunning Strike effects on Opportunity Attacks (in general, Steady Aim interacts nicely with Opportunity Attacks already, see Thief 2024).  But that’s rather subtle.  

A compromise: focus on *bonus actions* and *Sneak Attack triggers* for offense, not on many Reactions.  Maybe one Reaction ability (Counter Strike) at an early subclass level, and rely on Uncanny Dodge for defense.  And encourage use of Disengage/Hazard Reaction (Opportunity Attacks) as normal.  

We will consider giving at most **one new Reaction** (e.g. riposte on miss) to the subclass, and clearly note that it competes with Uncanny Dodge and OA.  

## **Skills and Noncombat**  

Bartitsu was as much about preparation and awareness as raw fighting.  We should tie in some skills:  

- **Perception/Insight:** A Bartitsu rogue should notice danger early.  Perhaps expertise in Perception or at least proficiency reflects this.  For example, a feature: “You have proficiency in either Insight or Perception” (like Outlander’s alertness).  Or simply rely on the Rogue’s class skill choices.  Thematically: reading opponents, hearing footsteps, spotting hidden weapons.  

- **Athletics/Acrobatics:** Acro is Dexterity with body – fits the nimble fighter.  Athletics is Strength-based but used for climbs and jumps (less Bartitsu-specific).  I’d lean Acrobatics (ease of movement, tumbles).  A niche: using Acrobatics for certain grapples (see grappling above).  Perhaps give Acrobatics as a class skill if not already.  

- **Sleight of Hand:** Concealing small objects (like easily hidden knives).  Maybe minor flavor (conceal a shiv or acquire a weapon).

- **Investigation:** Making connections, planning – not obviously historical, but detectives like Holmes are heavy on Investigation.  Could be taken as background.  

- **Medicine/Anatomy:** Knowing where to strike joints or senses might suggest a nod to Medicine or a special proficiency (though probably too heavy).  Possibly allow a one-time "Insightful Strike": if you roll expertise on Medicine or Heal, temporarily you know one weak point?  This is stretching it.  Better: "You have proficiency in Medicine" as flavor (like lifeguard).  

- **Social (Persuasion/Intimidation):** Historical Bartitsu did not rely on intimidation (pride of strength) – it was more about surprising attackers.  Persuasion could be flavor (“I am no match for you, stand down” bluff).  Intimidation might fit if using a steely glare.  Not core, can be normal Rogue picks.  

- **Misc:** Knowledge (Street) – not official, but idea that Bartitsu is “street-wise.”  Perception + Insight probably covers it.

We should ensure the subclass isn’t obligated to pump any non-Dex stat.  Though genre-wise, Intelligence (“scientific”) is not a must (Bart-W himself had Technical Ed).  Making Intelligence important would be misleading; leave it Dex/Cha or Dex/Wis.  

Probably let the player choose expertise in two skills as usual – they can pick from Acrobatics, Insight, Perception, maybe Athletics.  But we might suggest Acrobatics and Perception or Insight as thematic defaults.  

## **Design Pillars**  

From the above, we can articulate 3–5 core design pillars (broad themes for the subclass):

1. **Eclectic Range Mastery:** A Bartitsu Rogue moves fluidly among weapon ranges – cane (reach), unarmed/kick (short), and interacts at mid-range (kicking/punching) and close range (grappling).  
   - *Justification:* Bartitsu’s essence is combining stick, boxing, grappling seamlessly.  
   - *Experience:* The player should feel they have options to attack, trip, or evade at all distances.  
   - *Existing Mechanic:* Cunning Strike (Trip, Withdraw), Weapon Mastery (Topple on cane), Cunning Action (Disengage via Withdraw), Expertise (Acrobatics for safe movement).  
   - *Supporting Mechanics:* Allowing cane as finesse (Sneak Attack at reach), Cunning Strike options (Trip/Withdraw), maybe a bonus to Acrobatics checks for jumps.  
   - *Undermining:* Relying only on one style (e.g. only grappling), or no mix of cane vs fists would violate this.

2. **Scientific Leverage:** The Rogue should exploit physiology and timing, not raw force. Use precise strikes and locks to debilitate.  
   - *Justification:* Barton-Wright emphasized leverage, balance disruption, joint locks.  
   - *Experience:* The player uses timing and calculated effects rather than brute damage.  
   - *Existing Mechanic:* Sneak Attack (precision hits), Cunning Strike (effects like Knock Prone, Poison), maybe a Dex-based grapple rule.  
   - *Supporting Mechanics:* Allow unarmed strikes with Dex for damage, use Acrobatics to grapple, focus on saving throw-based control effects (forcing target saves).  
   - *Undermining:* Granting high Strength attacks, or giving buffs to damage instead of effects.  Also avoid turns where the rogue must spam attacks — want quality over quantity.

3. **Control the Initiative (Reactive Play):** Encourage striking first and responding to attacks.  This includes ambushing, using reactions, and moving after attacks.  
   - *Justification:* “Surprise him before he regains balance”, plus Holmes-esque countering.  
   - *Experience:* The rogue often goes first (Steady Aim) and punishes being attacked.  
   - *Existing Mechanic:* Steady Aim (ensure first hit), Cunning Action (Hide to set up an attack), potential Reaction (Counter Strike on miss).  
   - *Supporting Mechanics:* Possibly Reaction features for counterattacks.  Sneak Attack on first turn or after a missed enemy attack.  
   - *Undermining:* Passive play or solely Defensive Action; also overlapping too many reactions with Uncanny Dodge could conflict.

4. **Exploit the Opening:** Leverage Sneak Attack and Cunning Strike to *finish or disable* the foe.  Emphasis on turning any hit into a crippling blow.  
   - *Justification:* Bartitsu always aims to end the fight once an opponent is vulnerable.  
   - *Experience:* Whenever the rogue lands Sneak Attack, they can choose an impactful effect.  Combos of effects should allow finishing a foe quickly.  
   - *Existing Mechanic:* Cunning Strike (adds debuffs on Sneak Attack).  Bonus Action Cunning Action to escape after attack.  
   - *Supporting Mechanics:* Ensure the subclass has strong choices for Sneak Attack enhancements (Knock Out, Daze, Trap).  Perhaps allow spending dice to restrain or disarm.  
   - *Undermining:* Making Sneak Attack purely damage-focused or limiting Cunning Strikes; or focusing on buffs rather than debuffs.

5. **Always Prepared:** A Bartitsu Rogue is resourceful, using mundane tools and surroundings to fight.  They should not rely on magical gear or rare weapons.  
   - *Justification:* The cane is ordinary and self-defense should work for anyone with a stick.  Bartitsu clubs often used chairs, umbrellas in demos.  
   - *Experience:* The subclass should not require exotic items; features should work with any club/staff, basic knives, etc.  Possibly allow improvised weapons to qualify for Sneak Attack.  
   - *Existing Mechanic:* Rogue proficiency with simple weapons.  Steady Aim and Cunning Strike work with any attacks.  
   - *Supporting Mechanics:* E.g. grant Sneak Attack on improvised clubs or allow use of items in environment (Combat with objects? minimal mechanical needed).  
   - *Undermining:* Mandating a special magic cane, or making all features require a cane in hand.  Should allow fighting with fists/boots if no weapon.

These pillars will guide which mechanics we include at each tier and what to avoid.

## **Balance Hazards**

Several potent interactions must be scrutinized:

- **Sneak Attack expansion:** Allowing Sneak Attack with clubs/quarterstaffs (i.e. cane) or unarmed greatly increases options, but does it break DPR?  Likely not excessively – rogues can already get SA with daggers, shortswords, etc.  Giving SA to a club adds one 1d6 weapon option (like a mace but finesse).  Unarmed (1d4 base) is minor damage, so SA use is viable only if no other weapon.  Danger arises if multiple weapons can all SA and the rogue can use multiple Attacks (but Rogue has only one attack).  So expanding SA’s domain mostly affects flavor/availability, not damage scaling.  The real increase is convenience more than damage.  We should ensure not also giving Extra Attack, to avoid compounding.  

- **Dexterity Grappling:** A high-Dex, Expertise Rogue who can grapple with Acrobatics could restrain foes cheaply (Expertise Acrobatics + Prof + Dex ~ +13 at high level).  Combine with Advantage (Hide), this might make grapples automatic.  Once grappled, a rogue can sneak on them every turn with advantage effectively.  This could rival Monk disarms.  We must compare: Monk or Fighter grapplers usually use Athletics+Adv.  Rogue’s Expertise might overmatch.  Perhaps require Sneak Attack be spent to grapple (essentially making it a Cunning Strike effect), or set a cap (only Small/Medium).  

- **Reaction Attacks:** If we allow a free attack on enemy miss, that gives extra opportunities to trigger Sneak (once per round limit still).  But if the rogue uses Steady Aim first round, hits, then on enemy’s turn gets an OA, that could be potent.  Need to limit: maybe only trigger if you didn’t already take an Action that turn (so normal OA rules), or only cost a resource.  The rule “Cunning Strike uses Sneak dice” might not apply to this, so it's tricky.  Must ensure Rogue doesn’t get to sneak-attack outside its own turn too frequently.  

- **Cunning Strike economy:** Forgoing damage dice for effects must remain balanced.  Reducing SA die for e.g. Knock Out (6d6) should be rare; by L14 a rogue has maybe 7d6, spending 6 on one effect is big.  But this is a choice.  We should just align with 2024 design (heavy effect costs heavy dice).  

- **Action economy:** Rogues already have strong Bonus Action usage (Cunning Action, bonus attacks via Weapon Mastery, etc.).  Adding e.g. an extra attack Reaction or bonus (Nick mastery) must avoid giving Rogue multiple bonus attacks plus SA.  It seems Weapon Mastery Nick (extra attack if light weapon in offhand) may conflict with using cane (cane is neither light nor Finesse normally).  Nick is for light weapons – Rogues often pick Dagger anyway.  So can ignore Nick since cane is heavier.  

- **Multiclass issues:** A Bartitsu Rogue dipping into Monk could stack Dex grappling with Martial Arts dice.  If the Bartitsu subclass gave Dex unarmed normally, plus Monk UA, risk double-dipping Dex.  Also Rogue-X classes: if Fighter or Barbarian multiclass, combining Rage (Str requirement) with Dex focus is odd.  Probably fine.  But careful: Fighter 1 for Second Wind + cane proficiency, Monk 1 for Unarmored Defense (we should avoid giving similar features ourselves).  

We’ll note these hazards under “What to Avoid” or “Open Questions” and ensure our recommendations mitigate them (e.g. requiring grapples use Sneak dice or limiting some effects).  

## **Comparable Precedents**

Official mechanics with similar flavor may guide us:

- **Battlemaster Maneuvers (Fighter):** Cleave, Trip, Disarm, Parry, Riposte.  We won’t copy, but our Counter Strike reaction is akin to *Riposte*.  Our Trip CS is like *Trip Attack*.  Our proposed Disarm CS resembles *Disarming Attack*.  These use targets’ Saves and spend damage dice, very similar to Cunning Strike.  We should ensure our CS “costs” are on par (1d6–6d6 range).  

- **Swashbuckler (UA/forged):** Though not official, the Swashbuckler Rogue (UA or e.g. from 2017) had *Rakish Audacity* and *Panache* to add similar melee flair.  Particularly, *Panache* could charm or frighten targets that miss you.  Notably, the Battlerager Dwarf or Sword Coast Adventurer (SCAG) had Battlemaster Carousing etc.  The *Bladesinger Wizard* uses Mastery of Defense (similar to AC).  But nothing in 2024 Rogue exactly matches physical disarms or parries.  

- **Mastermind (UA Rogue):** Introduced “Insightful Strike” (secretly record target’s AC) – not relevant to Bartitsu.  

- **Monk (2020, revised):** Dexterous Attacks – Monks can use Dex for Str-based weapons.  This exists in the core as a Monk feature.  We consider similar.  
- **Master Martial Artist (2023 Unearthed Arcana Fighter):** Allowed disarm & trip; not official, but shows WotC interest in these.  

- **Knife Master feat (Tasha’s):** Allows celerity with thrown weapons; not directly relevant.  

- **Polearm Master feat:** Lets you attack with butt-end – reminiscent of cane.  If rogues had that feat, they'd get cane butt-attack.  But our subclass could mimic that without requiring a feat.  

These precedents suggest that adding Disarm/Trip as Sneak uses is not out of line, and an extra Reaction attack (Riposte) is analogous to Fighter’s Riposte maneuver.  Using Dex for unarmed or special proficiency on cane is analogous to Monk’s flexibility feats.

## **What *Not* to Become**  

Misleading approaches to avoid:

- **Kung Fu Superhero:** No mystical inner power or wis-dollar-power. Bartitsu is hard martial training, not qi.  Avoid extra attack chains (Flurry of Blows), deflect missiles (monk style), or instant knocks (beyond our few CS options).  

- **Pure Jujitsu Style:** Don’t focus solely on grappling/holds. Bartitsu was broad – a cane or punch-centered culture.  A subclass focusing only on grappling would be one-note.  

- **Only Cane Fighting:** The cane is important, but the subclass should work unarmed too.  For example, “if you ever drop your cane, you’re useless” must be avoided.  So features should not all require cane.  

- **Sherlock Holmes Spoof:** Ironic or clueless detective tropes (like requiring Int “clever detective” checks) are goofy.  We want the serious fighter-scientist vibe, not forcing Intelligence checks.  

- **Giant Barbarian Supremacy:** Avoid any feature that lets the rogue ignore strength contests entirely.  They still should struggle against huge brutes or giant monsters in meaningful ways.  

- **Setting Lock:** Not every game is Victorian London.  If someone wants a Bartitsu Rogue in a Forgotten Realms or Eberron, they should still fit (i.e. they can say “combination fighting style with cane/staff,” even if style was named differently).  So avoid Victorian-only lore requirements.  

- **Mandatory New Resource:** Do not introduce a separate “Bartitsu Ki pool” or “initiative tokens.”  Stick with existing Action/BA/Reaction economy.  

- **Significant Stat Skew:** Don’t force heavy Strength or Intelligence dependency (e.g. no stat prerequisites beyond normal Rogue). Intelligence was important to Barton-Wright personally, but not needed for every fighter of his style.  

- **Neo-Bartitsu as Fact:** If something is mostly modern imagination (like exotic throws from Judo that Barton-Wright never learned), we should not treat it as granted knowledge.  It can be a *possibly learnable* stance, but we should label it as such.  

Mechanically, these mean:  
- Avoid scaling Monk attributes (Martial Arts die, Unarmored Defense).  
- Avoid too many free disengages or invisibilities.  
- Avoid heavy AoE or ranged skills (Bart wasn’t about magic).  
- Avoid requiring a creature to be smaller (Rogues already can’t trip Huge, but if we add things, consider how it works vs large targets).  
- Avoid making the subclass only work with a cane: it should function with any simple weapon or unarmed, given the prep.  

## **Final Design Recommendations**  

1. **Core Fantasy (2–4 sentences):**  
   *“A Bartitsu Rogue is a master of eclectic self-defense who fights with a scientific precision.  At range they wield a cane (or club), at midrange they throw punches and kicks, and at close range they grapple and lock joints – all while staying nimble and opportunistic.  They read their opponent’s moves and exploit openings, turning an attacker’s aggression into their downfall.  In flavor, they are the resourceful adventurer or detective who carries only mundane tools but strikes with deadly skill.”*  

2. **Essential Pillars (ranked):**  
   1. **Eclectic Range Mastery:** Transition among cane, boxing, kicks, and throws seamlessly. *(Top priority; core Bartitsu trait.)*  
   2. **Scientific Leverage:** Use timing, positioning, and anatomy to overcome stronger foes (e.g. trips, joint locks, balance disruption). *(High priority; distinguishing feature.)*  
   3. **Exploit the Opening:** Make your Sneak Attack *also* a tactical move – stuns, trips, disarms. *(High priority; reinforces archetype synergy.)*  
   4. **Control the Initiative:** Strike first when possible and penalize opponents’ attacks (guards, counters, movement). *(Moderate; supports the reactive style.)*  
   5. **Always Prepared:** Use only basic arms and surroundings; no dependency on magic or exotic items. *(Moderate; flavor guardrail.)*  

3. **Essential Bartitsu Elements (must-include):**  
   - Ability to use a cane/club or unarmed with Dexterity (for weapon finesse & Sneak Attack).  
   - Cunning Strike options or similar for trip/prone, disarm (if chosen), and withdraw/speed control.  
   - A “counter” or reactive strike feature (e.g. reaction attack on missed blow) to reflect Bartitsu counters.  
   - Acrobatics or Dexterity-based grappling nuances.  
   - Skills like Perception, Insight, Acrobatics to represent awareness and agility.  

4. **Optional Elements (good but skippable):**  
   - Extra reaction parry (since Rogue already has Uncanny Dodge).  
   - Any minor magical disguise skills.  
   - Stealth-specific boosts beyond normal (Bartitsu had no formal stealth component).  
   - Additional fancy cane mastery beyond Topple/Slow (we have those via Weapon Mastery).  

5. **Things to Avoid (to preserve identity):**  
   - Turning the subclass into a Ki-using or Extra-Attack subclass (Monkification).  
   - Forcing high Strength or Intelligence scores for attack or defense.  
   - Overloading the Reaction economy (e.g. avoiding a cascade where Counter Strike and Uncanny Dodge always conflict).  
   - Making grapple or unarmed mechanics so powerful the Rogue outperforms Monks in that domain.  
   - Having every feature require the cane (should be useful unarmed and with daggers etc. too).  

6. **Recommended Mechanic Foundations (most→least promising):**  
   1. **Sneak Attack (with Cunning Strike):** Core damage source; use it to apply Bartitsu effects (trip, disarm, daze).  
   2. **Weapon Mastery (Topple/Slow):** Leverage 2024 mechanics so the cane/staff has built-in prone/slow effects when mastered.  
   3. **Cunning Action:** Maneuverability (withdraw, hide) is very Bartitsu, especially the Withdraw Cunning Strike.  
   4. **Reactions:** Possibly one or two reactive strikes or parries, carefully limited.  
   5. **Skills (Expertise):** Dexterity (Acrobatics) and Perception/Insight for flavor, not a formal “mechanic”, but part of the character kit.  
   (Sneak Attack and Cunning Strike top the list because they are the established way for Rogues to deal damage and apply status.)  

7. **Cane Recommendation:**  
   The cleanest solution is to treat an ordinary cane (club or quarterstaff) as a finesse weapon for the Bartitsu Rogue.  Specifically, a feature (say at level 3) could state: *“You may use your Dexterity instead of Strength for attack and damage rolls with club or quarterstaff, and such attacks qualify for Sneak Attack.”*  This lets the Rogue use their signature cane without new rules or magic items.  They still use Weapon Mastery normally for Topple/Slow.  This approach also works seamlessly in any setting (just say “staff or cane”).  

8. **Unarmed/Grappling Recommendation:**  
   Introduce a minor rule change or feature: *“When you make a grapple or shove attempt, you may use Acrobatics (Dex) instead of Athletics (Str) and use your Dexterity in the DC.”*  Coupled with allowing Dex for unarmed strikes (like a simplified Monk martial arts), this ensures Rogues can effectively use joint-lock strategies.  This should be carefully tested for balance with Expertise: we might limit it (e.g. only after a successful attack or as a Cunning Strike option).  But as a baseline, freeing grappling from Strength aligns with historical Bartitsu leverage.  

9. **Feature Identities:**  
   - **Level 3 Feature:** *Cane-fighting training.* Enables using cane/club as finesse (Dex) and a simple Reaction “Riposte” (when an enemy misses you within 5 ft, you can make one cane or unarmed attack).  This embodies learning the new art and Vigny’s counter-stroke principle.  
   - **Level 9 Feature:** *Counter-technique specialization.* Possibly enhance the Reaction (e.g. make it use Sneak Attack automatically or give extra damage) or add a new Cunning Strike like “Disarm/Restrict”.  Historically, after mastery comes refinement of counters.  For example: an option to force a Strength (or Dex) save to drop weapon when you hit with Sneak Attack.  
   - **Level 13 Feature:** *Blitz and defense.* Provide a way to hit multiple foes or ignore one incoming attack.  For example, “Unpredictable Movement”: once per short rest, when you miss or are missed by a foe, you can immediately move (no OA) and make a Strike; or you can use a Reaction to move out of melee without provoking.  This reflects continued work on evasion and preemptive moves.  
   - **Level 17 Feature:** *Martial perfection.* A powerful capstone.  Maybe “Perfect Balance”: when you would be stunned/prone/knocked back by an attack, you succeed on the save (or half it) automatically.  And once per turn, when you hit with Sneak Attack, you can roll all effect dice as max (represent ultimate precision).  This is analogous to Stormmasters (crit) but focusing on control effects.  

(These are **conceptual goals**: exact wording would need playtesting.  But the idea is: mid levels reinforce the pillars of counters and cunning strikes; capstone solidifies survival and finishing power.)

10. **Design Thesis:**  
   *“A successful Bartitsu Rogue should feel like a disciplined self-defense strategist using precision and deception, **not** like a mystical martial artist relying on inner power.”*

This concludes the research and design-reference briefing for a Bartitsu-inspired Rogue subclass.  By combining the cited historical evidence with the 2024 Rogue framework, a subclass developer has a roadmap for features that are both authentic to Barton-Wright’s eclectic system and mechanically fitting for D&D 2024.
